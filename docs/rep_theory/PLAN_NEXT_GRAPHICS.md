## 2. How the Planche video is built (the pattern, not this topic)

1. **Hook in 10 s: a bold claim plus the proof device.** "If you can hold a plank for 30 seconds, you can do a planche. I'll
   prove it." The hero picture has a handwritten aside "(stupid easy)" and an arrow. Then "A planche isn't a strength trick"
   (the question is shown, then **struck out**), "It's a lever": a lever diagram with a pivot ring, a weight arrow, a
   distance bracket and a vertical **LOAD meter** that fills.
2. **Day 1 vs the goal.** The failed first attempt (speed lines), then the goal card at the end of "the road".
3. **The spine of the whole video: a 7-link progression chain.** Seven cards with an icon, a lock and a target ("30 S",
   "3 × 15 S", "3 RULES"), a warning card at link 4, "HAVE IT ✓" over link 1, "GOAL" over link 7, and a dotted "YOU" line.
   It comes back **at every new unit**: the current link is lit, done links are ticked, and later it shows "UNLOCKED" and
   "YOU". It also returns in the result and at the end. This is the single biggest device we lack.
4. **The early test plus the comment ask**, locked until the end ("At the end I'll tell you which link you start on").
5. **The physics unit ("why skinny wins"), every claim drawn as a device:**
   - load = weight × distance, as a formula box beside the lever;
   - "+10 kg", as the backpack pinned on the far end of the lever;
   - thickness vs weight, as a circle vs a cube ("BIGGER ×1.5");
   - a study card and a line graph;
   - weight vs strength meters;
   - grey silhouettes of elite gymnasts with measurements (167 cm, 65 kg);
   - a height chart ("didn't make them short");
   - a bathroom scale ("+1 POWER-UP").
6. **Units (links 1-7), always in the same order:**
   - the chain zooms to the link, then the title;
   - the setup cues, each on the same picture with an arrow, ring or label on its word;
   - the muscle (a back diagram of the shoulder blades, an x-ray arm);
   - a study card;
   - **the number**, as tokens ("3 × 15 S" plus three circles) or a big stopwatch with its target;
   - the next link teased.
7. **The warning unit** has hazard tape across the screen, x-ray tendons, a shaded **gap graph** (muscle strength runs ahead
   of tendon strength), "FEEL READY ≠ BE READY", and **3 rules** as slots that fill and come back with ticks.
8. **The mistakes section:** "3 MISTAKES" as numbered cards. Each mistake gets its own section label ("MISTAKE 1 · BENT
   ELBOWS") with wrong (red X) vs right (green ✓). At the end all three come back as a ticked list.
9. **"Train it like a skill":**
   - tear-off calendar pages ("FEW months" vs "1-2 years, SOME NEVER");
   - a calendar of one brutal day vs spread out;
   - a split circle STRENGTH | BALANCE;
   - a rest battery plus a week strip.
10. **The plan:** a clipboard built row by row (warm-up, current link, previous link, push-ups), "4 DAYS A WEEK ≈ 15 MIN",
    a hold chain with rest timers, and "2 sessions clean → unlock the next link" with a padlock opening.
11. **The result:** bands, each pointing to a link on the chain. Then a comment mock "8 s → link 2. See you at link 6"
    with an arrow to that card, and a callback to the trainee with the chain: "ONE PLANK AWAY FROM STARTING".

Text system: section label per unit, small white lead-in plus coloured key word (we have this now), a safety chip during
every exercise unit (we have it, but it rarely fires), and hazard tape for warnings.

## 4. How a new script gets its graphics (the answer to "each script is new")

The rule stays the same: **the graphic is chosen by what the sentence does, not by its topic**. A planche script, a
sleep script and a protein script all have counts, levels, a test, warnings, a lever-like trade-off, numbers and a plan. A
new script gets new *content* in the same *devices*. Four layers, in this order:

### Layer 1: code-drawn devices filled from the narration (most of the video, free, exact, animated)
Each device in groups A-D is a **component with a trigger and parameters** read from the sentence. Some examples:

| Sentence shape (any topic) | Device | Parameters taken from the words |
|---|---|---|
| "N links / levels / steps / stages from X to Y" + later "Link 3. The …" | progression chain | N, each unit's name and target, the warning unit |
| "X times / multiplied by Y", "the further … the heavier" | lever + formula box | the two factors, the object named |
| "too far forward / back", "balance", "seesaw" | seesaw | the two sides named |
| "N sets of M seconds / reps", "hold for N", "rest N" | tokens, hold chain | N, M, rest |
| "N% / half / two thirds of your weight" | gauge with mini figure | the shares said |
| "X grows faster than Y", "the gap" | gap / area graph | the two things, the time unit |
| "few months … years", "some never" | tear-off calendar pages | the durations said |
| "N rules / mistakes" | slots, then a ticked recall later | N and the names said |
| "rest", "recover", "tendons rebuild" | battery + week strip | the days said |
| a question to the viewer ("How many seconds…? Comment") | test lock, then result bands at the end | the bands said |

The parameters always come from the script, so no two videos look the same. When a device does not fit, the scene falls
back to a picture or a card (that's how it works today).

### Layer 2: Flow pictures of the character and the scene (one per moment, re-framed 2-4 times)
This works today: the hook, the exercise setups, before/after pairs, with labels, arrows and lines on top.

### Layer 3: Flow-made art assets, made once and kept in the library
For things code cannot draw well (an x-ray limb, the shoulder blades from behind, a brain and spine, a backpack, a scale,
a plate of food), the tool asks Flow for a **clean art asset with no text** (ink style, black background, the part in red)
the first time a script needs it. It stores the asset in the library under its name (`xray_forearm`, `back_shoulder_blades`,
`obj_backpack`…) and **reuses it in every later video**. The library grows with each topic, so the Flow cost is paid once
per asset, not once per video (the same rule as the pose library).

### Layer 4: text, numbers and motion always drawn in code, on top of Flow art
**My recommendation on "generate graphics with Flow and merge them with the narration":** use Flow for the **art**, never
for the **information**.
- Flow (an image model) cannot be trusted to write exact numbers, words or units, and a number baked into a picture can't
  count up, tick, fill or land on the spoken word.
- A full infographic slide made by Flow would be a still picture: nothing would happen on the narrator's words, and every
  new number would need a new generation.
- So Flow draws the drawable part (the x-ray arm, the backpack, the silhouette, the scale). The code draws the labels,
  arrows, numbers, meters and timing on top, at the exact word. This is the same split that made the picture scenes work
  today (Flow picture + code labels, arrows and lines).

### How the CSV directs it
The CSV prompt gains an optional **`graphic` column** where the script writer can name a device and fill it in one line,
for example:
- `chain: plank 30s | lean 3x15s | frog stand 30s | WARNING tendons | lift-off 6x2s | tuck planche 10s | full planche`
- `lever: weight=your body | pivot=your hands | object=+10 kg backpack`
- `gauge: knee push-up 50% | push-up 66% | planche 100%`
- `asset: xray forearm` (asks for a library asset)

A blank cell keeps the app's own detection. The `graphic` column always wins over the app's choice, and the app checks it
against the sentence, so no numbers are invented (the same rule as `on_screen`).

### Coverage report (so we know what to add next)
After every render the log lists **sentences no device covered** (they fell back to a plain picture). Over a few scripts
this shows which device to build next, from real scripts instead of guesses.

## 5. Never losing what we have
1. **Feature count check, built first:** for all 14 test scripts it records how often each of the ~47 scene types and ~33
   effects appears. Any change that drops one to zero, or sharply, fails the tests before a render.
2. **Every new device is additive**: its own sentence-shape trigger, one more option in the rotation (never the default),
   all existing overlays still drawn on it, its finish time registered (`cards.CARD_DONE`), its own test, and its own line
   in the feature count.
3. **One group at a time**, then my own preview render of a real script with a before/after sheet, then a commit and tag
   as a restore point. No parallel helpers without asking first.

## 6. Build order (proposal)

| Phase | What | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Feature count check + coverage report | protects everything we have; shows what is missing per script |
| 1 | Progression chain tracker (+ callbacks, unlock, recalled ticked slots), `graphic` column in the CSV prompt | the spine of both videos; turns any script with units into a guided journey |
| 2 | Meters and numbers: LOAD meter, gauge with mini figure, big stopwatch with target, sets/holds tokens, hold chain, effort bar + dot matrix | every fitness script has numbers; cheap, exact, animated |
| 3 | Diagrams: lever + formula box, seesaw, gap/area graph, split circle, two-panel compare, tear-off calendar, battery + week strip, clipboard plan, road | the "explain why" moments |
| 4 | On-picture tricks: handwritten aside + arrow, struck-out question, measurements, hazard tape, speech bubble, silhouettes | cheap polish on pictures we already show |
| 5 | Flow asset library (x-ray limbs, back view, brain/spine, top-view hands, objects, mockups) + questions list with Q tracker | needs Flow credits; made once, reused forever |

After each phase: tests + feature count + my own preview of a new script (not only the arm-wrestling one) + a sheet,
then commit and tag.
