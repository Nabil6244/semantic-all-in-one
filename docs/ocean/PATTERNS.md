# Ocean Predators style — reference study (ApexMorph, all 7 videos)

Studied 2026-11-14: channel ApexMorph (earlier brand name "ApexFauna"), 6.7K subs, 7 videos
(every upload on the channel). Each video: 360p copy, auto captions, 30-frame sheet, hard-cut count.
Goal: adopt the channel's repeatable PATTERNS as blueprints our planner applies to any
script. We draw our own animals, write our own scripts and use our own clips — no copied art.

## 2. Script grammar (identical in all 7 — make it a template)

1. **Cold open, second person**: every video starts with "Picture this. You're …"
   (floating off California / you're an orca / holding a fossil tooth). 3–5 punchy facts
   that build the hero up ("nothing hunts it").
2. **The turn**: "Except …" — one shocking specific (a year, a count, a place).
   "They didn't die, they ran."
3. **Promise line**: "So today we're finding out …" (≈30–60 s in).
4. **Myth bust**: "When most people think about X, they bring up A, B or maybe C.
   And yes, … But …" — knocks down the obvious answers, sets the rule for the list.
5. **Ranked list / zones** — 4–6 chapters, each one animal or one depth zone, usually
   escalating ("the most obvious and honestly the most awkward" → the scariest).
   Each chapter: named study/place/year → number → mechanism → a joke comparison
   ("that's like saying mice keep cats away").
6. **"What would you do?" chapter** (in 6/7 videos): back to second person — the viewer
   in the water / as the orca, with game-like choices.
7. **Big-picture close**: humans / climate / "why we're seeing more of this now",
   ending on a question → comments card → "Watch next".

Chapters are named in the description with timestamps (6–10 per video).
Credits line in the description lists every CC clip/photo source.

## 3. The world (visual system)

- **One home stage**: deep-blue water gradient, darker towards the bottom, soft god-rays
  from the surface, a few floating specks. ~60 % of screen time is on this stage.
- **Stage variants** (same drawing kit, different set): surface line with a dark
  island/coast horizon; sandy sea floor cross-section (wavy water line, sand bottom);
  kelp forest; abyss (near black, purple tint); split screen "around Antarctica vs
  everywhere else" water columns; depth gauge column with a ruler (surface, 500 m …).
- **Flat 2D animal drawings**, side-on, thick dark outline, 2–3 tone shading, cartoon eyes.
  They swim in slow loops, turn, open jaws, school in groups, form defensive circles
  (sperm whale "marguerite" star). Size comparisons put two animals on one line with
  a measure bar (~16 m vs ~11 m) or next to a car / a diver.
- **Close-ups** of one part drawn big: suckers with hooks, an eye, a tooth on a
  spotlight pedestal, barnacle skin.
- **Silhouettes**: a black shape for the "unknown" animal before the reveal.
- **Real footage, short**: 1 in ~6 shots, 3–6 s, desaturated / tinted to sit in the blue
  world, always a tiny credit line bottom-left (NOAA, Wikimedia CC, Pexels). Used for
  "this is real" moments: first filmed alive, seal colony, a fin at the surface.
- **Globe**: flat stylised Earth in space, a region lit up / ringed (Antarctic current).
- **Flat maps**: teal sea, sand land, yellow hand-drawn circle around a place, dotted
  migration route, red dots for incidents.

## 4. Text system

- **Caption = one short phrase per beat**, not full subtitles. Small white lowercase
  words + one or two key words in **bold uppercase orange** (yellow-orange #F5A623-ish;
  red for danger words: WIPING OUT, GONE, HOT TUB, KILL).
  Bottom-centre usually; top-centre for a section idea ("THE DOLPHIN THING").
- **Number hero card**: huge orange number centred + small line under it with an
  orange key phrase ("16 / didn't come back UNTIL NEXT YEAR"). Ranges: "10–18 TONNES".
- **Label callouts**: thin white leader lines from body parts with small caps labels
  (MASSIVE SHOULDERS, ~1/3 OF THE BODY IS HEAD).
- **Name tags**: species name top-left, small, underlined (Otodus chubutensis).
- Font: one rounded geometric sans, bold for keywords. No boxes behind text.

## 5. Card / gag library (the hits use the most of these)

Data cards
- Dot-grid / icon grid: 90 dots, red ones = the rare outcome ("90 real attacks / 5 seals caught").
- Icon row count: rows of little whale icons ("FEWER THAN 80").
- Two-number standoff: "35 ORCAS" left vs "9 SPERM WHALES" right with the animals between.
- Progress bar with % ("~93 % of pre-whaling numbers"), horizontal bar race (top speed).
- Meter panel: LIGHT / FOOD / OXYGEN bars falling as depth number rises.
- Counter in a corner: "0 APEX PREDATORS LEFT", clock "0:30 HRS", calendar page "NOV 1997".
- Timeline strata: tooth fossils stacked by age (~7.6 / ~14.5 / 20–17 million yrs).
- Family line: name chips in a row (grandpa / dad).

Story / gag cards
- Mystery line-up: 3 cards with "?" + 01 02 03, revealed one by one as the list goes on
  (returns between chapters to show progress).
- Research paper card (title + author, greyed body lines) for the cited study.
- Game menu "CHOOSE YOUR MOVE" (FIGHT / OUTLAST / HUNT QUIETLY / SWIM AWAY) for the
  "what would you do" chapter; quiz card "DOES THE HUMPBACK… EAT THEM? NEED THEM?".
- Coach playbook (chalk Xs, dotted arrows, "1. SEPARATE").
- Crime-scene tape, WANTED poster ("REWARD: ONE VERY LONG GRUDGE"), halo + sparkles,
  party bunting, online-shop listing ("$249.99 ADD TO CART"), museum tag, evidence marker.
- Predator vs victim two-panel cards; "Flight species" silhouette chart on paper.
- Diver HUD: depth "12 m", air bar, tip line ("RISE SLOWLY, FACING IT").
- End: comments card with a fake viewer question, "WHICH ANIMAL NEXT?", logo sting,
  "WATCH NEXT" end-screen frame.

Rule seen across videos: **a gag card every ~45–60 s**, data card every ~30 s,
real footage every ~40 s, everything else is the drawn stage with animals acting the line.

## 6. Pacing and sound (from captions/frames; audio not measured)

- New hard shot every 6–11 s, but caption/beat change every 2–4 s.
- Shots follow the sentence: the noun being said is on screen at that moment
  (said "dolphin" → dolphin swims in).
- Chapter starts: a title-ish top caption or a stage change (surface → depth, day → night).
- Music bed low under voice, soft underwater ambience; whoosh on card pops (to confirm by ear).

## 7. Build plan (new "Ocean" style — reuse, don't rebuild)

Base: the Aviation sticker-story engine (aviation/: stage + stickers + story planner +
cards + sound) already does "fixed stage + flat drawings acting the line + cards".
Ocean = a new theme + kit on top of it, plus a few new card types. Steps:

1. **Stage kit**: underwater home stage (gradient, god rays, specks, slow drift),
   surface/island, sea floor, kelp, abyss, depth-column, split columns, globe, flat map.
2. **Animal sticker library**: our own flat drawings, made once, reused by every video
   (shark, great white, orca, humpback, sperm whale, pilot whale, dolphin, seal,
   squid types, megalodon, diver, boat, cage, tooth, fossil). Each with swim loop,
   turn, jaw-open, school mode. Pictures made via Flow (needs your OK) or drawn as
   vector; never their art.
3. **Caption system**: one-phrase captions with keyword picking (number, superlative,
   danger word → orange/red), top vs bottom placement rule.
4. **Card pack**: number hero, dot grid, icon rows, standoff, bar/meter, counters,
   calendar/clock, strata, name chips, labels, mystery line-up, paper, choose-your-move,
   quiz, playbook, gag props (tape/poster/halo/shop/tag), diver HUD, comments, watch-next.
5. **Planner rules**: sentence shape → visual (named animal → sticker acts it; number →
   number card; "X vs Y" → standoff/size bar; study/year → paper/calendar; "you" →
   diver HUD / game menu; list chapter start → mystery line-up). Quotas from §5.
6. **Real clips**: short tinted clips with auto credit line, ~1 in 6 shots, via the
   Visuals tab as every style does.
7. **Script template + CSV**: the 7-part grammar in §2 as a writing template (for the
   CSV/script skill), chapters + credits auto-written to the description.
8. **Test** on 3+ new-topic scripts (not just sharks) before calling it done.

## 9. How their animals move (reverse-engineered 2026-11-14, 720p frame-by-frame)

Studied the walking tiger in "The Deadliest Man-Eaters" (7:57-8:05) and the swimming orca in "The Only Animals That
Make Orcas Run Away" (8:06), at 30 fps.

- **Vector puppets, not pictures.** Every animal is a set of separate flat vector shapes: body, head, ears, each leg,
  tail, fins. Fills are flat colour with one soft gradient (lighter belly), crisp edges, a few detail shapes (stripes,
  white cheek / belly patch, eye), sometimes a thin light rim line. No fur, no painted texture, no 3D shading.
- **Jointed legs.** Each leg is two segments (thigh + shin, upper arm + forearm) with a bend at the knee / hock, white
  paw at the end. The near legs carry the body colour and stripes; the far legs are one darker solid brown, drawn
  behind the body. Legs cycle in a four-beat walk; the knees bend as the leg lifts and swings forward.
- **Walk in place.** The animal stays near the middle of the frame and walks on the spot over a static dark set
  (a treadmill cycle). Crossing the frame is done by slowly moving the whole puppet, never by sliding a still.
- **Secondary motion.** The body bobs slightly with the steps, the head nods, the tail is a chain of segments
  (striped bands on the tiger) that sways in a wave. Soft ellipse shadow under the feet.
- **Swim cycle (orca).** Flat body + white patch + dorsal fin + flipper; the tail flukes beat up and down, the
  flipper paddles, the body drifts slowly forward with a gentle up/down. Same flat-fill look.
- Implies an After Effects-style puppet rig (shape layers + joints / IK) with looping cycles per animal type.
