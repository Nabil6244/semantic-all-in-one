Why it misses (the code-level causes)

  1. The director only reacts to numbers with units and a few keywords. Dates ("early 2027", "November 27th"), named publications ("Android Headlines"), reliability words ("leak", "rumor", "medium reliability"), contradictions, verdicts ("wait", "the risk") and questions get no picture. So 22 sentences fall back to a generic 3D phone or room.
  2. Context from the sentence before is lost. "The S26 Ultra has 5,000, so that's about 700 more, roughly 14 percent" has no "mAh", and "percent" isn't parsed as %. So the battery comparison gets nothing, even though the previous sentence was about mAh.
  3. A CSV filler can steal a better scene. The two CSV taglines go on sentences that have "no scene", but that's decided before context is known. "Less. But more" took the battery comparison line. The Privacy feature also took the model-lineup sentence (Pro/Ultra vs S27/Plus) instead of being shown together with it.
  4. Some cards drop the very figure the sentence says. The 3C document shows no "5,534 mAh", and the quote card says "As reported" instead of naming "Ice Universe". The name-matching rule only accepts a capitalised name after "tipster" and misses this one.
  5. Filler 3D isn't tied to the topic. Under a battery sentence it can show a port macro or a room lineup. Moves rotate in a fixed order, not by subject (battery → x-ray cutaway, camera → island, design → edge/profile, chip → chip).
  6. The script's own structure isn't used. "First, the design. This one is a leak." / "Second, the cameras. This one is a rumor." / "Third, the battery. Certification." That's a section label with a reliability tag, which the references show and the planner ignores.
  7. Readability. "Privacy" big type sits behind the phone, and one flat phone card has an empty title.
