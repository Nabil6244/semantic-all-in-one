What the root problem is

  1. The CSV was read as the script. The trailer CSV has 12 lines (#product, #disclaimer, the header, 9 rows). The run treated each line as a narration sentence, which is where "12 scenes for 12 sentences" comes from, and why the subtitles read #product, #disclaimer, order, 1 … 9.
  2. Because of that, the timing broke. That "script" never matched the voice, so the run logged "the voice-over did not follow the script word for word: lines were spread evenly", and every block got exactly 17.53 s. Whisper had already transcribed all 518 words with timings, but none of it was used to place scenes.
  3. The CSV values never reached the screen. 200MP, 50MP 5x, 5700 mAh, 60W, Privacy and Snapdragon Ultra appear nowhere. The wash:red, wash:purple, horizon and badge looks were ignored.
  4. An AI scene-writer filled the gap with made-up content. One Gemini call (gemini-3.6-flash) wrote the scenes even though a CSV was supplied, which goes against the "CSV means no Gemini" rule. That's where 120Hz, Snapdragon 8 Gen 3, Geekbench 7200/6500, the vapor chamber, the charging curve and the flex hinge came from.
  5. The product line was ignored. #product,Galaxy S27 Ultra,Titanium Black wasn't picked up. The product name is empty in the spec, so cards show "Phone 1" and the lineup shows 4 colours, not Titanium Black.
  6. Footage was thin and wrong. One Pexels search found one clip (an iPhone-style phone in a yellow case). It's looped for the whole 17.5 s opening.
  7. Long still screens. 11 of 21 shots are "black" shots with 2D paper cards. The same flat phone card is held for 18 s, and the same battery card for 17 s, which breaks the no-still-screens rule.
  8. Small layout bug. The disclaimer end card's single long line overflows the frame.
