# Reference check 5: "Samsung S27 Ultra - The Design CHANGED Again!" (studied 2026-11-06)

11:17, 1280x640, 29.97 fps. A different creator from reference 4: a presenter at a desk, a round presenter bubble bottom-right on
every other shot, a sponsor segment (their own app). Same method as reference 4: faster-whisper transcript, one frame every 2 s, scene cuts.

## Rhythm
- 222 cuts, median shot **2.4 s** (reference 4 was 3.6-5.1 s). Inside a source card the picture still changes every 1-2 s.
- Rough mix: ~45 % source cards (tweets, articles, a Reddit comment, a Weibo post, a maker's slide), ~30 % renders of the phones,
  ~20 % presenter, ~5 % real footage (hands, an event, a commercial).
- The video is cut into **named sections** ("Another Redesign", "Regular Models", "Camera Specs", "Colours", "Chips & RAM",
  "Battery Capacity", "Polar ID", "Release Date & Price"); the current section's name stays bottom-left on every shot.

## What this adds to reference 4
Confirms gaps already listed: the hook montage (12), opinions on something other than a graphic (11), the karaoke mark (9, and here it
also walks sentence to sentence and word to word), the ring (5, built), colours in spoken order (3, built), figures on their word (1, built).

New patterns (numbered after reference 4's 14 gaps):
15. **Section tag**: the current chapter's name bottom-left on every shot, changing when the section changes (the script's `##` chapters,
    or a topic change the director detects).
16. **Source card v2 (article / post)**: the source's name above a framed card on a dark moving backdrop; the spoken sentences are
    marked one after another as they are read (sentence karaoke), then the camera pushes in on the marked line. Works for an article
    paragraph, a headline, a post, a list. Our `pt_tweet` / `pt_doc` do one fixed mark.
17. ~~**Two-sided figures**~~ **built 2026-11-06** (`product_trailer/sides.py`, `layers/pt_sides.mjs`; 3D hero phone, or a flat phone on
    the paper in infographic / news; every other such sentence keeps the older flat scenes; "from X" not built): two big figures with a small line under each, either side of one phone ("5500mAh | 6000mAh",
    "$1299 S26 Ultra Launch Price | $1499 S27 Ultra Leaked Price", "LPDDR5 Not LPDDR6 | UFS 4.0 Not UFS 5.0"); each lands on its word.
    Includes the **"Not X"** line for a downgrade and the **"from X"** line for a change ("218g / from 214g").
18. **Generations row**: every past model in a row (S20 Ultra … S26 Ultra) with the same figure popping above each in turn, for
    "ever since the S20", "seven generations in a row". In 3D: our lineup room with N phones.
19. **Viewer prompt pill**: "let me know which … you prefer" -> a speech pill at the bottom with that question.
20. **Earlier-video card**: "in our last video" / "we talked about this back in July" -> a card for the channel's earlier video
    (title from the script or the CSV; no real screenshot).
21. **Sample photo card**: talk about photo quality ("warmer photos", "bad in low light") -> a sample picture inside a phone frame
    with a label ("S26 Ultra / 200MP Main"); the picture from stock / Flow, never invented as a real sample.
22. **Slide box**: on a maker's slide (or any card), a box frames the name being said, then moves to the next name.

Not adopted: the presenter and the presenter bubble (no presenter in our films), the sponsor segment, real commercials.

## Where 3D replaces their renders
Their renders are pre-made stills with a slow push. Ours: the hero phone moves (`island` for "camera plateau", `profile` for
"thickness 7.9mm", `hero_back` for the weight figure), the lineup room for "four models" and the generations row, two phones for
"S27 Ultra | iPhone 17 Pro" side by side; the two-sided figures (17) sit either side of a 3D phone.
