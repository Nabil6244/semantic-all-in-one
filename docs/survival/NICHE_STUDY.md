## Script template (current)
1. **Cold open, 15-30 s**: a concrete scene or a claim that flips a belief. "That 40-lb bag in your closet was packed by somebody who wanted your money." / "Texas doesn't even make the top 20." / "The 70-year-old with bad knees often outlasts the 25-year-old with a $1,000 backpack."
2. **One lens for the whole video**: "the verb test" (a tool must give your hands a verb they can't do bare), "think in layers", "every state lives on what it already has".
3. **Tease #1 with a strange detail**: "some of its power plants are fed by a conveyor belt from the mine next door"; "researchers found grown adults giving themselves electric shocks before 15 minutes".
4. **Tease a middle item with a cost**: "Number seven costs $300 if you get it wrong, and you won't know until you're already sick."
5. **Countdown units** (60-120 s each):
   - second-person scene: "Night one on the walk home. Try fixing anything in the dark with a flashlight in your mouth."
   - the wrong version they sold you vs the fix
   - simple science with one number: "water draws heat from your body about 25 times faster than air"
   - 2-3 features or steps
   - "One trick for anyone who already owns one" / "And this one is for every viewer, wherever you live"
   - a dry punchline: "There's a special kind of insult in sitting in a dark house under your own solar panels."
   - bridge into the next item
6. **Ending**: back to the lens, then **homework tonight** ("dump your bag on the floor", "run one evening with the main breaker off", "put the phone down for an hour"), then a **forced-choice comment question** ("You can only carry four of the seven. Which three stay home?"), then a **subscribe joke** ("Nine in 10 people who watch a gear video never subscribe. And that's how they keep buying the wrong gear."), then the **next video tease**, then one last callback line.
Persona variant (40k): one real-feeling old man, "Ray", 81, a barn in Western Pennsylvania, four real emergencies, nine things he buys every October; each item told through him. The comment question asks about the viewer's grandparents.
Old template (80k drinks video): generic, safety disclaimer, subscribe pitch at 1:30. It still did well: **title and topic get the click; the newer writing keeps people watching.**

## Full script the user shared: thermal drones, "$20 trick" (Fallout Lab / Boring Apocalypse)
Not a countdown. **One secret held to the end**, with chapters that each knock down a belief:
1. Hook: the threat in one picture (a drone finds you in minutes) → the belief it breaks (a bush or a tarp hides you) → **the promise** ("a way to hide for about $20, and it starts with something already in a lot of first aid kits") → **a twist on the promise** ("most people who own one would use it in the exact way that gets them spotted").
2. How it works in plain words (98° body vs near-freezing ground; "a warm spot where a warm spot shouldn't be").
3. Proof it's real: Ukraine drones; farmers flying thermal drones to find fawns in hay fields (a vivid, surprising picture).
4. Myths, each one wrong for a reason: camo jacket ("a person-shaped light bulb"), bush ("bushes don't have warm middles"), tarp (works for minutes, then draws your outline), coat, family huddle.
5. **The rule**: "Cover only works if it stays at the temperature of its surroundings." Then **a reminder of the open loop**: "Hold on to that because the $20 trick is built around beating exactly that."
6. Surprises: glass blocks thermal ("a dark mirror"); but warm rooms, roofs and chimneys glow; heat prints (where you sat, a car's hood, ashes, a generator, the dog).
7. **Subscribe used as a tease**: "If you just pictured your own roof on that screen, hit subscribe because next is the hour most people would pick to slip away unseen. And it's the hour you glow the brightest."
8. Timing (the hour before sunrise is the worst; a warm evening and hard rain are the best), then place (one thick pine beats a bare forest; walls, banks, overhangs).
9. **Honesty line**: "I'll be honest, military cameras are very good, and nothing makes you invisible for hours."
10. **Payoff at about 85%**: "Remember that thing sitting in a lot of first aid kits?" The emergency blanket (NASA, 1960s, about 97%). Two wrong ways (touching you = glowing patches; shiny outside = a cold rectangle with straight edges), then the fix (air gap with the shiny side in + a ground-matching outer layer), the cost ($2 + wool or burlap = about $20), then two limits (heat builds up, straight edges).
11. Homework (try it in the backyard with the kids; ask a hunter to look at you through a thermal scope), a one-line rule, a forced-choice question (early evening or cold rain, and why?), next video tease, final callback line.
Craft: every chapter ends on a line that opens the next one ("Some ordinary things block the camera completely and one of them is in every house. It surprises almost everybody."). Strong mental pictures every 20-30 s (light bulb, dark mirror, chain-link fence, the warm print of your backside in the dirt, a chimney like a flare). Family stakes throughout (kids, the huddle, the dog on a leash).
For us: a 6th format, **"one secret, many myths"**. The visuals want a **thermal view**: our own Flow picture shown in eye view, then switched to a heat-camera palette (white-hot / ironbow) in code, so the "person-shaped light bulb" can actually be shown. Split screen eye vs thermal, the warm print fading, the blanket wrong vs right.

## What our Survival style needs (from this study)
Not one format but five, all on the same look:
- **Countdown list** (most videos): number card, item info strip (cost / takes / difficulty), progress strip, #1 tracker.
- **Place ranking** (states, cities, places): a US map that lights the state, rank badge, weak spot + fix card. We already have map engines.
- **Timeline** ("first 72 hours", "hour by hour"): a clock / day strip card that advances.
- **Persona story** (an old-timer the whole video follows): one recurring character in every picture (Flow reference images, like Jung and Elder casts).
- **Gear myth vs truth** ("they lied"): the sold version vs the fix side by side, price compare.
Script prompt must teach: cold open, lens, two teases, unit grammar, homework, forced-choice question, subscribe joke, next tease. Facts must come from the script; safety warnings stay in (water, food, medicine, CO from heaters).
