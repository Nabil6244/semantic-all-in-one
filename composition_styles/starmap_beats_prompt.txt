You are writing the BEAT PLAN for the "StarMap" style of Semantic YT Studio: a space
documentary that alternates between a real 3D MAP OF SPACE (the real Sun, planets, Moon and
stars in their true positions for the date of the story, with one camera that glides from
place to place, from a launch pad to a lunar landing site to the whole galaxy) and
full-screen FOOTAGE (real NASA video and photographs). On the map the app draws titles,
markers on surfaces, landing zones, spacecraft, their flight paths and orbits, number chips,
distances, captions and photo cards. The map explains WHERE, WHEN and HOW FAR. Footage
shows WHAT IT WAS LIKE.

You cut the narration into BEATS. A beat is one stretch of the story, told one way:
  map          the map of space, with its layers
  map_footage  the map, with one to three PHOTO CARDS on it (the map says where, the cards show what)
  footage      full-screen clips or photos (one to three), the map waits underneath
Under each beat you list what appears in it. The app times everything from the
narrator's own words, flies the camera for you, moves the universe to the right date, and
renders the video.

OUTPUT
Output ONLY the CSV, starting with the header row. No explanations, no code fences, no
blank lines. Quote any cell that contains a comma.

HEADER (exactly this, in this order)
beat,row,vo_anchor,mode,type,id,place,frame,date,label,sub,text,value_from,value_to,format,anchor,until,hold,asset,dur,why

ROWS
The FIRST row after the header is the plan row: beat=plan, row=plan, id = the mission pack
(given at the end of this prompt; leave empty if there is none), label = the video's title.
Then every row says which beat it belongs to (beat = b1, b2, b3 ... in order) and what it is
(row = beat, layer, card or clip). Write the beat row first, then that beat's rows.

- row=beat: opens a beat.
    text       the beat's narration, its whole sentence or sentences, copied EXACTLY from the
               script (every word, in order). Beats start at the start of a sentence.
    vo_anchor  the first 3 to 5 words of that text, copied exactly
    mode       map, map_footage or footage
    place      (map beats) what the camera looks at: a body (earth, moon, sun, mars, jupiter,
               saturn, pluto, alpha centauri, sirius, barnard's star, wolf 359, milky way,
               andromeda, large magellanic cloud, triangulum), a pair (earth+moon,
               sun+alpha centauri, milky way+andromeda), or a SITE from the mission pack or the
               sites list below
    frame      (map beats) how much we see:
                 surface  low over a site, its craters and the horizon (place = a site)
                 close    a large part of the body, or a site from high up
                 body     the whole body (a site then faces the camera)
                 system   a body with its moon, e.g. place=earth+moon or moon
                 inner    the inner planets around the Sun
                 solar    the whole solar system
                 galaxy   the Milky Way from outside (its centre, arms and "you are here"
                          are labelled by themselves); with milky way+andromeda, both
                 universe the whole observable universe, galaxies by the thousand
    type       (map beats, optional) push_in (slow zoom in), pull_out, orbit (the camera
               circles), hold
    date       (map beats) the moment in time the beat shows: an EVENT of the mission pack
               (launch, landing ...), an event plus or minus time (landing-00:12:40,
               tli+02:00), mission time (T+102:45:40), or a date like 1969-07-20T20:17:40Z.
               The universe moves to it: when two beats have dates, time runs smoothly from one
               to the next, so the spacecraft flies along its path between them. Give a date
               whenever the story's moment changes; leave it empty to keep time running.
    why        one short phrase: what this beat does ("where they launched", "the long coast")
  A beat ends where the next beat starts. The first beat starts the video.

- row=layer: something drawn on the map (map and map_footage beats only).
    vo_anchor  the words it appears on: 2 to 5 words copied from ITS OWN BEAT'S text cell
    type       title, marker, zone, craft, path, orbit, stat, caption, distance, line,
               rings or pointer
    and what that type needs (see LAYERS)
    until      leave empty (it leaves with its beat), after_footage (still there when the
               map comes back after the next footage), end (until the video ends), or a beat
               id such as b7 (until that beat ends)
    hold       seconds, only to make it leave sooner

- row=card: a photo card of a map_footage beat (one to three per beat).
    vo_anchor  the words it appears on, from ITS OWN BEAT'S text cell
    anchor     where on the screen: tr (default for the first), ml, mr, bl. Cards on screen
               together get DIFFERENT places
    hold       seconds on screen (default 6)
    asset      nasa_image:<specific description> or stock_image:<specific description> for a photo;
               nasa_video:<...> or stock_video:<...> for a card that plays a short clip (a turning planet,
               a flare, a flyover) while the map stays up
    label      a short name in capitals shown under the card; sub: an optional second line

- row=clip: a clip of a footage beat (one to three per footage beat, in order).
    asset      nasa_video:<description>, stock_video:<description>, nasa_image: or
               stock_image: for a still
    why        what this clip shows that the map cannot
    dur        optional seconds; leave empty and the beat's time is shared between its clips

HOW TIME WORKS (the most important rule: a CSV that breaks it does not load)
The app finds every vo_anchor in the recorded narration, beat by beat, in order. So:
- THE BEATS COVER THE SCRIPT EXACTLY. Put the text cells of all beats one after another
  and you get the script, word for word: nothing skipped, repeated, reworded or added.
- A beat's vo_anchor is the FIRST 3 to 5 words of its own text. If those words also appear
  earlier in the script, take more words until the phrase is unique.
- A layer's or card's vo_anchor is copied from ITS OWN BEAT'S text cell, nowhere else. When
  a thing is first named in the NEXT beat's text, put its row under the next beat.
- Copy words exactly as the script writes them: same spelling, same numbers (digits or
  words, as the script has them). Never paraphrase or shorten a name.
- Avoid anchoring on common short words alone ("the Moon", "and then"): use 3 or more words,
  or a name spoken only once in that beat.
- Spoken pace is about 2.5 words per second, so a beat of 25 words lasts about 10 s.

THE STARMAP RHYTHM
- Alternate: map, footage, map again. Footage is usually 25 to 45 percent of the running
  time. Do not leave the map up for more than about 25 seconds without a card or footage.
- MAP when the narration is about where, when or how far: a launch site, an orbit, a
  journey, a distance, a date, a landing site, the scale of space. FOOTAGE when it
  describes something filmed or photographed: a launch, the crew, a cabin, a moonwalk,
  a splashdown. MAP_FOOTAGE when a named thing needs a picture but where it is still matters.
- A footage beat is 5 to 12 seconds, one to three clips. A map beat is usually 8 to 20
  seconds. Do not switch mode every sentence.
- THE MAP MUST KEEP BUILDING: something new appears at least every 5 seconds of map
  narration (a craft, a path, an orbit, a marker, a zone, a number, a card, a caption).
- Show the journey: when the narration follows a spacecraft, give the beat the craft and
  its path, and a date, so the viewer watches it travel. When it reaches a place (orbit, a
  landing site), switch place and frame in a new beat.
- Use a stat chip for each real number the narrator says (a distance, an altitude, a
  speed, a count). When that number is a distance or a light travel time between two
  things, ALSO draw it with a line row (light for light travel times, measure otherwise),
  so the map shows what the number means. Use a caption for a short key phrase (at most 44
  characters), rarely.
- Do not put two stats in the same corner at once; a stat and a caption not within 4 s.

CAMERA
- You do not write camera moves. Give each map or map_footage beat its place, frame and
  date; the app flies the camera between beats (never under footage) and lights every
  body from the Sun's side.
- Keep the same place and frame for beats that stay in the same view, so the camera does
  not move for nothing.

LAYERS
- title: label (the video or part title), sub optional. One per part; never repeat.
- marker: place = a SITE (from the lists below, or body@longitude,latitude such as
  moon@23.47,0.67), label = its name in capitals (optional: the site's own name is used),
  sub = a short second line.
- zone: place = a SITE; value_to = the radius in km (default 25); label (e.g. LANDING ZONE).
- craft: id = a CRAFT of the mission pack; label = the name to show (optional).
- path: id = a PATH of the mission pack (a stretch of a flight, drawn as the craft flies it;
  give the craft too).
- orbit: id = an ORBIT of the mission pack (the ring the craft circles on).
- stat: value_to (the number stated in the script), value_from (optional), format with ONE
  number placeholder written with 0 or # and the unit after it ("#,##0 KM", "0.0 KM/S").
  NEVER type the number itself in the format. sub = the small caption. anchor: bl, br, tl.
- caption: text, at most 44 characters.
- distance: place = two things separated by ; (a body, a site or a craft, e.g. earth;csm),
  sub = what it measures (FROM EARTH). It counts live as the craft moves, and a dashed line
  joins the two.
- line: SHOWS a distance on the map. place = two things separated by ; (moon;earth), or one
  body to measure across (milky way). id = light (a glowing line with light running from the
  first thing to the second: use it whenever the script says how long light takes) or
  measure (a dashed measuring line). label = what it says (1.3 LIGHT-SECONDS, 2.5 MILLION
  LIGHT-YEARS). One line at a time is the focus: give each its own moment.
- rings: scale rings around a body, so the viewer feels a jump in scale. place = the body
  (sun), text = the sizes separated by ; (1 light-hour; 1 light-day; 1 light-year), each
  optionally size=LABEL. Give them until = the next beat when the camera flies outwards
  through them.
- pointer: a pin with a label on a thing (place = sun, label = YOU ARE HERE), for a view
  from far away.

PICTURES AND CLIPS (asset)
- Do NOT invent file names. Describe what to find: nasa_image:<description>,
  nasa_video:<description>, stock_image: or stock_video:. Concrete and visual ("Saturn V
  lifting off from the launch pad", not "rocket"). Prefer nasa_ for real missions, and name the
  mission in the description ("Apollo 11 astronaut beside the flag on the Moon") so the picture is
  from the right one.
- Do not repeat the same description or idea twice.

ACCURACY
- Show only what the narrator says. Never add facts, numbers, places or dates that are not
  in the script or in the mission pack below.
- Use only the events, sites, craft, paths and orbits listed below (or the bodies named in
  this prompt). Anything else cannot be drawn.

LONG SCRIPTS
For a long script, write it in parts if you must: continue the beat numbers (b41, b42 ...)
and do not repeat the header or the plan row. Each part starts with the sentence right after
the last beat's text of the part before. The parts are pasted together into one file.

CHECKLIST BEFORE YOU ANSWER
1. The header is exactly as given, then the plan row; every row has the same number of cells.
2. Beats are numbered b1, b2, b3 ... in order; each beat row comes before its rows.
3. TIME: joining every beat's text gives the script word for word. Every beat's vo_anchor
   is the first 3 to 5 words of its own text and unique so far. Every layer's and card's
   vo_anchor is found, word for word, in its own beat's text cell.
4. Every map and map_footage beat has a place and frame, and a date when the moment changes.
5. Every map_footage beat has one to three cards in different places; every footage beat one
   to three clips and nothing else.
6. Every event, site, craft, path and orbit is one listed below; every number is from the script.
7. Pictures are nasa_/stock_ descriptions, never file names.
8. Output is the CSV only.
