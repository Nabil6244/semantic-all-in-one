You are writing a visual-plan CSV for the "Exp Solar" style of Semantic YT Studio.

THE LOOK
A plain white whiteboard canvas, like a reference documentary: photo and video cards
appear while the narrator speaks. Most moments show one strong HERO card; some show two
cards side by side, a reaction next to what it reacts to, four cards in a row, or a grid
of up to 15 small cards. A chapter title stays in the header while its subject is
discussed, and a short checklist can stay at the top of the screen. Arrows are rare.

OUTPUT
Output ONLY the CSV, starting with the header row. No explanations, no code fences, no
blank lines. Quote any field that contains a comma.

COLUMNS (exactly these, in this order)
scene_number,script_segment,node_id,beat,role,asset_type,prompt,caption,highlight,chapter,node_label,relationship_to,relationship_type,relationship_label

- scene_number: 1, 2, 3 ... one per row, no gaps.
- script_segment: the narration for this row, copied WORD FOR WORD from the script.
- node_id: the card this row adds: write n followed by the scene_number (row 7 -> n7).
  Leave it empty on a narration-only row.
- beat: how the card is shown (see BEATS). Empty means hero.
- role: leave empty (the beat sets it).
- asset_type: where the card's media comes from:
    commons_image  real named things: a specific person, place, building, event, species
    stock_image    generic photos (money, hands, a skyline, a silhouette)
    stock_video    generic footage (driving, engines, crowds, nature)
    youtube_video, archive_video, nasa_video, commons_video   real footage
    image / video  AI-generated, only when nothing real exists (costs Flow credits)
    map            an animated map; the prompt is the place, big to small: "Italy > Modena"
- prompt: a short, specific search (6 to 12 words for stock) or a generation prompt.
  Never a person's name in a stock search, never a brand, never a file name.
- caption: the spoken words of THIS row, trimmed to 10 words or fewer. Copy the words;
  do not paraphrase or summarize. Leave empty on narration-only rows.
- highlight: one or two key names, numbers or terms from the caption, separated by a
  comma. Each must appear EXACTLY (same letters, same capitals) inside the caption.
- chapter: the title of a NEW subject, only on that subject's first row (see CHAPTERS).
- node_label: a 1 to 3 word tag shown above the card (optional); required on a
  checklist row (the item's name).
- relationship_to, relationship_type, relationship_label: link this card to a card
  already on screen (see LINKS). Usually empty.

HOW TIME WORKS (the most important rule)
- THE ROWS COVER THE SCRIPT EXACTLY. Put the script_segment cells one after another and
  you get the script, word for word: nothing skipped, nothing repeated, nothing reworded,
  nothing added. Use the script exactly as the narrator recorded it.
- One row is about 2.5 to 3.5 seconds of speech (roughly 7 to 10 words). Split long
  sentences at natural pauses.

NARRATION-ONLY ROWS (holding a card)
When the narration keeps talking about what is already on screen, write a row with ONLY
scene_number and script_segment filled and every other column empty. The cards on
screen stay. Use these freely to keep every row at 2.5 to 3.5 seconds.

BEATS
- hero        (default) one strong card on its own. Most rows.
- title       the opening card of a subject: a clean, recognizable picture of the
              subject itself. Put the subject's chapter on this row.
- two_panel   two cards side by side: write TWO two_panel rows in a row; they pair up.
              For a comparison, before/after, two views of one thing.
- reaction    a reaction picture (a face, a crowd, a gesture) beside what it reacts to:
              set relationship_to to that card's node_id.
- four_row    two to four cards in a row: write two to four four_row rows in a row.
              For a short list or a quick transition through examples.
- index_grid  a grid of up to 15 small cards: write up to 15 index_grid rows in a row.
              Only for a long list the narrator names item by item.
- checklist   one item of a checklist that stays at the top of the screen for the whole
              video (at most 15 items in all). Fill node_label with the item's name.
              Usually the first row of each new part of a list-style video.
- cta         the closing card that asks the viewer to act (subscribe, watch next).

CHAPTERS
- A subject is the thing being explained; most videos have 1 to 4 subjects.
- Put chapter (a short title) on the subject's FIRST row only, usually a title beat.
  It stays in the header until the next chapter. Never repeat a chapter.

LINKS (use sparingly: most rows have none)
- relationship_to = the node_id of a card that is still on screen, so the two show
  together.
- relationship_type: leave EMPTY for a simple pairing (a reaction, a supporting
  detail). Fill it only for a real cause and effect: causation, dependency,
  consequence, transformation, mechanism or process. That draws an arrow: at most one
  arrow in every five or six cards.
- relationship_label: 1 or 2 words on the arrow, only when they add meaning.
- two_panel, four_row and index_grid rows are joined automatically: leave their links
  empty.

CHECK BEFORE ANSWERING
1. Joining every script_segment in order gives back the script exactly.
2. Every card row has node_id n<scene_number>; narration-only rows have only
   scene_number and script_segment.
3. beat is one of: hero, title, two_panel, reaction, four_row, index_grid, checklist,
   cta (or empty). two_panel rows come in pairs; four_row in runs of 2 to 4;
   index_grid in runs of up to 15.
4. relationship_type is empty or one of: causation, dependency, consequence,
   transformation, mechanism, process; relationship_to names a card already on screen.
5. chapter only on each subject's first row; every checklist row has a node_label.
6. Every highlight appears exactly inside its caption.
7. Rows average about 2.5 to 3.5 seconds of speech (7 to 10 words).
8. Output is the CSV only.
