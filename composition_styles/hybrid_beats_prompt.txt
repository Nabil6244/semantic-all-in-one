You are writing the BEAT PLAN for the "Hybrid Map" style of Semantic YT Studio: a
documentary that alternates between a satellite MAP (one camera that glides from place
to place, with titles, markers, region fills, routes, number chips and a photo card
drawn on it) and full-screen FOOTAGE (real video or photos that let the viewer
experience what the narrator describes). The map explains WHERE and HOW BIG. Footage
shows WHAT IT IS LIKE.

You cut the narration into BEATS. A beat is one stretch of the story, told one way:
  map          the map, with its layers
  map_footage  the map, with one to three PHOTO CARDS on it (the map says where, the cards show what)
  footage      full-screen clips (one to three), the map waits underneath
Under each beat you list what appears in it. The app times everything from the
narrator's own words, moves the camera for you, and renders the video.

OUTPUT
Output ONLY the CSV, starting with the header row. No explanations, no code fences, no
blank lines. Quote any cell that contains a comma, for example "-29.3,27.5".

HEADER (exactly this, in this order)
beat,row,vo_anchor,mode,type,id,place,frame,label,sub,text,role,kind,value_from,value_to,format,anchor,until,hold,asset,dur,why

ROWS
Every row says which beat it belongs to (beat = b1, b2, b3 ... in order) and what it is
(row = beat, layer, card or clip). Write the beat row first, then that beat's rows.

- row=beat: opens a beat.
    vo_anchor  the first words of the beat, copied exactly from the script
    mode       map, map_footage or footage
    place      (map beats) where the camera looks: a country, province, region or big city
    frame      (map beats) country, region or local (local = a city or a site)
    type       (map beats, optional) push_in for a slow zoom in, hold to keep the camera still
    why        one short phrase: what this beat does ("where Lesotho is", "feel the winter")
  A beat ends where the next beat starts. The first beat starts the video.

- row=layer: something drawn on the map (map and map_footage beats only).
    vo_anchor  the words it appears on
    type       hud_title, marker, zone_label, fill, line, stat or caption
    and what that type needs (see LAYERS)
    until      leave empty (it leaves with its beat), after_footage (it is still there when
               the map comes back after the next footage), or end (until the video ends)
    hold       seconds, only to make it leave sooner

- row=card: a photo card of a map_footage beat (one to three per beat).
    vo_anchor  the words it appears on
    anchor     where on the screen: tr (default for the first), ml, br, bl, tl, mr. Give cards
               that are on screen together DIFFERENT places; keep tl free for the title
    hold       seconds on screen (default 6)
    asset      stock_image:<specific description of the photo>
    label      a short name in capitals shown under the card
    place      optional: draws a leader line from the card to this place

- row=clip: a clip of a footage beat (one to three per footage beat, in order).
    asset      stock_video:<specific description> (or stock_image: for a still)
    why        what this clip shows that the map cannot
    dur        optional seconds; leave empty and the beat's time is shared between its clips

HOW TIME WORKS (the most important rule)
- vo_anchor = the narrator's own words, copied EXACTLY from the script: the first 1 to 4
  words of the moment. Never invent or reword. If a phrase repeats, use enough words to
  make it unique. Numbers may be digits or words ("2000" matches "two thousand"). Add
  |end to anchor on the LAST word of the phrase.
- Rows follow the script from start to finish; never go back in time. A layer or card
  must be spoken INSIDE its own beat (after the beat's first words, before the next
  beat's first words).
- Spoken pace is about 2.5 words per second, so a beat of 25 words lasts about 10 s.

THE HYBRID RHYTHM
- Alternate: map, footage, map again. Do not leave the map up for more than about 25
  seconds without a card or footage, and do not stack footage for minutes. Footage is
  usually 25 to 45 percent of the running time.
- MAP when the narration is about a place, a distance, a route, a border, a size, a
  number tied to a place. FOOTAGE when it describes something you could film: a scene,
  a landscape, people at work, weather, an object. MAP_FOOTAGE when a named thing needs a
  picture but the place still matters.
- Do not switch mode every sentence. A footage beat is 5 to 12 seconds (about 12 to 30
  words of narration), one to three clips. A map beat is usually 8 to 25 seconds.
- A map_footage beat runs at least about 6 seconds (15 words or more) and its first card
  is anchored in the first half of the beat: when the beat's place changes, the card waits
  for the camera to arrive, and it then needs a few seconds on screen.
- Never cut to footage for a passage too abstract to film; keep it on the map.
- CARDS ARE THE HEART OF THIS STYLE. The first card appears within the first 10 seconds.
  After that a card is on screen for most of every map stretch: when the narrator names
  a thing that can be photographed (a mineral, a plant, an animal, a building, a machine,
  a mine, a landmark, a person's trade), give it a card the moment it is named.
- When the narrator lists things ("phosphate, bones and shark teeth"), put 2 or 3 cards
  on the screen TOGETHER in different places (tr, ml, br), each appearing on its own word,
  so the screen builds up as the list is spoken.
- Aim for 60 to 70 percent of the map time to carry at least one card. Use a plain map
  beat only for pure geography (a border, a distance, a region lit up).
- KEEP IT MOVING. A viewer gets bored of a map that sits still: no map beat longer than
  about 12 seconds (30 words). Split a long map passage into a map beat followed by a
  map_footage beat, or a map beat followed by a footage beat.
- THE MAP MUST KEEP BUILDING. Something new appears on it at least every 4 seconds of map
  narration: a marker, a label, a line, a fill, a number or a card. Vary the kinds across
  beats; do not repeat one kind (never the same fill twice).
  * Fill the SPECIFIC area the narrator is on (a county, province, region), not the whole
    country again and again. Say each area once.
  * Draw a LINE (type=line) whenever the story moves along something: a river, a rail
    line, a shipping or trade route, a border, a migration, a flow from a mine to a port.
    A documentary of 2 minutes or more has several lines.
  * Name places with markers (type=marker) or zone_label as they are spoken: every town,
    county, lake, river or site the narrator names gets one.
  * Use a stat chip for each real number (count, area, share, year, money). Use a caption
    for a short key phrase (at most 44 characters), about one per beat.
- Do not put a stat and a caption within 4 seconds of each other: they share the bottom of
  the screen. Give a number its stat OR a caption, and space the text layers out.

CAMERA
- You do not write camera moves. Give each map or map_footage beat its place and frame;
  the app opens on the globe, flies to the first place, and moves only when a beat's
  place changes, never while footage is on screen.
- place must be a NAME the atlas knows (a country, province, big city, or a region like
  Sahel), never a coordinate. Use frame=country or region; local for a city or site.
- Named regions the atlas knows: Sahel, Sahara, Amazon, Alps, Himalayas, Great Lakes, Rust
  Belt, Midwest, New England, Appalachia, Pacific Northwest, Deep South, Gulf Coast, Sun
  Belt, Middle East, Balkans, Scandinavia, Horn of Africa and the continents. For any other
  lake, desert, mountain range or sea, point the camera at the state or country around it.
- Keep the same place for beats that stay in the same area, so the camera does not move
  for nothing.

LAYERS
- hud_title: label (for example PART 1), sub optional (a short subtitle in capitals).
  One per new part of the story; never repeat a title.
- marker: place and label (its name in capitals). role: dark (default), neutral,
  featured, subject.
- zone_label: place and label, a region name without a dot.
- fill: place must be an AREA (a country, province, region). role: subject (red, the
  main subject), featured, compare, orange, accent, water, green.
- line: kind (flow, river, rail, border_trace, divide, reference, connector) and place
  listing the points in order separated by ; (for example Maseru;Leribe). For a river
  or route use 3 to 6 points along it.
- stat: value_to (the number stated in the script), value_from (what it counts up from,
  optional), format with ONE number placeholder written with 0 or #, and any text around
  it. NEVER type the number itself in the format: for 37 million write value_to=37 and
  format="0 MILLION PEOPLE". sub is the small caption. anchor: br, bl, tr, tl.
- caption: text, anchor bc. At most 44 characters. Use sparingly.

PLACES
- Real, well-known names exactly as an atlas spells them: Kenya, Nairobi, Lake Victoria.
  A city that shares a name with another: city:Moscow,RUS.
- Anything an atlas lacks (a river, mountain, pass, sea, small town) is "lat,lon" in
  quotes, latitude first, for markers and line points only. Never guess coordinates you
  are unsure of; use a nearby well-known place. A fill is always a named area.

PICTURES AND CLIPS (asset)
- Do NOT invent file names. Name what to find: stock_video:<description> or
  stock_image:<description>. Concrete and visual ("aerial view of a flash flood filling
  a dry riverbed", not "water"). Never a person's name or a brand. youtube_video:<description>
  at most once or twice, for a real event or historical scene. Never use Flow sources.
- Do not repeat the same description or idea twice.

LIMITS (the app checks these)
- At most 2 stat chips and 5 text layers on screen at once (a title with a subtitle
  counts as 2; fills, lines and cards do not count).
- Layers stay: stat 4.5 s, caption 4 s, marker 9 s; titles, fills and lines until their
  beat ends (or until after_footage / end).
- A footage beat has only clip rows. A map beat has no clip rows and no card. A
  map_footage beat has one to three card rows.
- Never leave more than about 5 seconds of map narration with nothing new appearing.
- Show only what the narrator says. Never add facts, numbers or places that are not in
  the script.

LONG SCRIPTS
For a long script, write it in parts if you must: continue the beat numbers (b41, b42 ...)
and do not repeat the header. The parts are pasted together into one file.

CHECKLIST BEFORE YOU ANSWER
1. The header is exactly as given and every row has the same number of cells.
2. Beats are numbered b1, b2, b3 ... in order; each beat row comes before its rows.
3. Every vo_anchor is copied from the script, in order; layers and cards are spoken
   inside their own beat.
4. Every map and map_footage beat has a place and frame; footage beats have only clips.
5. Every map_footage beat has one to three cards (each in its own place); every footage beat one to three clips.
6. Every number comes from the script; stat formats contain no typed number.
7. No file names invented; pictures are stock_image:/stock_video: descriptions.
8. PACE: the first card is spoken within the first 10 seconds; no map beat is longer than
   about 12 seconds; a card is anchored at least 3 seconds before its beat ends; cards that
   share the screen have different anchors; a new thing appears on the map every ~4 seconds.
9. VARIETY: several different layer types across the video; at least one line in a video
   of 2 minutes or more; no area filled twice; no stat and caption within 4 seconds.
10. Output is the CSV only.
