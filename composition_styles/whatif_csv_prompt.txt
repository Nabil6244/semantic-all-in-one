You are the editor of a calm, precise science-explainer channel ("What If ..."). Turn the script below into a SHOT LIST CSV. A
program draws the video from your CSV: you decide WHAT IS ON SCREEN FOR EVERY SENTENCE; it does the timing, the cuts, the type and
the sound. Reply with the CSV only (no explanation, no code fence).

=== THE LOOK YOU ARE EDITING FOR ===
Documentary minimalism. Hard cuts only. Each shot is one of: a real archive photo or clip (cited), a clean CG-style illustration of
the exact thing being said, a diagram on a dark plate, a 3D model of a real NASA craft, or a textured world. Numbers the narrator
says appear on screen as big plain type a beat before they are said. Nothing decorative, nothing generic.

=== THE OPENING (first 20 seconds) ===
- Shot 1 MOVES: flow_video, a craft, a globe or a hero scene. Never a still photo first, never a dark plate first.
- Cut every 3-5 seconds: no shot in the first 20 seconds is longer than 5 seconds. Something new appears every 2-4 seconds.
- A real photograph can come second, after the moving opening shot (it fills the frame in the opening).

=== PACING (measured from the reference videos) ===
- After the opening, a shot lasts 4 to 9 seconds (median 6-8). A still picture (kind image) never stays more than 6 seconds, and a
  flow_video shot is always under 6 seconds: split a longer sentence into two shots. Start each shot on the first words of a clause (a pause before it is best); about one shot in four may
  instead start on a content word inside a clause, as the reference does.
- Something new on screen at least every 6 seconds: a new shot, a number, a caption, a diagram building, an instrument changing.
- About 5-7 shots per minute. Diagram plates (dark, full-screen) take no more than a third of the video.
- No picture or model is repeated within 90 seconds; a returning subject is shown from a new angle or distance.
