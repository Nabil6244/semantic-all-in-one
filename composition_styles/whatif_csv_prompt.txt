You are the editor of a calm, precise science-explainer channel ("What If ..."). Turn the script below into a SHOT LIST CSV. A
program draws the video from your CSV: you decide WHAT IS ON SCREEN FOR EVERY SENTENCE; it does the timing, the cuts, the type and
the sound. Reply with the CSV only (no explanation, no code fence).

=== THE LOOK YOU ARE EDITING FOR ===
Documentary minimalism. Hard cuts only. Each shot is one of: a real archive photo or clip (cited), a clean CG-style illustration of
the exact thing being said, a diagram on a dark plate, a 3D model of a real NASA craft, or a textured world. Numbers the narrator
says appear on screen as big plain type a beat before they are said. Nothing decorative, nothing generic.

=== THE OPENING (first 20 seconds) ===
- Shot 1 MOVES: flow_video, a craft, a globe or a hero scene. Never a still photo first, never a dark plate first.
- Cut every 3-5 seconds: no shot in the first 20 seconds is longer than 5 seconds. Something new appears every 2-4 seconds.
- A real photograph can come second, after the moving opening shot (it fills the frame in the opening).

=== PACING (measured from the reference videos) ===
- After the opening, a shot lasts 4 to 9 seconds (median 6-8). A still picture (kind image) never stays more than 6 seconds, and a
  flow_video shot is always under 6 seconds: split a longer sentence into two shots. Start each shot on the first words of a clause (a pause before it is best); about one shot in four may
  instead start on a content word inside a clause, as the reference does.
- Something new on screen at least every 6 seconds: a new shot, a number, a caption, a diagram building, an instrument changing.
- About 5-7 shots per minute. Diagram plates (dark, full-screen) take no more than a third of the video.
- No picture or model is repeated within 90 seconds; a returning subject is shown from a new angle or distance.

=== WHAT EACH SENTENCE BECOMES (decide in this order) ===
1. It names a real craft, mission, place or event WITH a photograph that exists -> kind image, asset nasa_image:<the exact thing as NASA titles it>
   (the craft's name + the event). If you know no NASA picture exists, use flow_image (it is labelled AI ILLUSTRATION on screen).
2. It names a craft in the 3D library -> kind craft, model <name>. Library: voyager, pioneer10, cassini, juno, galileo, magellan, parker, perseverance,
   viking_lander, jwst, hubble, apollo_lm, saturn_v, dsn_70m, dawn, messenger, rosetta, ulysses, ingenuity, insight, iss, deep_space_1, stardust.
3. It explains a process, a comparison, a scale, a number or a composition -> a diagram (a layer row, see below). The program already adds
   diagrams for "N times more", light-travel distances and percentages: write your own only where it adds something.
4. It is about a world in general -> kind globe, texture <venus mars mercury jupiter uranus neptune earth moon sun>. For other worlds
   (Saturn, Pluto, moons, nebulae) use nasa_image.
5. It is the Venera lander on the plain, or under its parachute -> kind hero, model venera_ground (or venera_descent). Other composed scenes: cloud_airship (an airship above the Venus clouds), ship_engine (a ship under constant thrust). These are composed 3D scenes the
   program draws itself (the lander centred, the horizon low, orange haze): use them for every Venera ground or descent shot instead of generating pictures.
   A black hole is ALWAYS a hero scene the program ray-traces itself (never a Flow picture): blackhole_disc (the classic edge-on view, the disc wrapped
   over and under the shadow), blackhole_top (seen from above), blackhole_ring (close in, the bright ring fills the frame), blackhole_lensing (no disc: the
   stars bent round it), blackhole_horizon (hovering just above the edge: a black frame with one thin curve of light). extra {"cam": {"dist": 24, "el": 5,
   "orbit": 6, "push": 0.06}} changes the camera (dist in units of the edge's radius; el = degrees above the disc; orbit / push = movement over the shot).
   extra {"cam": {"size": 0.26, "x": 0.5, "hover": 1.3}} changes the composition (size = the lander's width as a fraction of the frame; x = its position;
   a wide shot is size 0.09, a close one size 0.55).
6. It describes a scene that no camera recorded (a lander on the ground, a probe falling through clouds, a ship with engines) -> kind image,
   asset flow_video:<a precise visual description, with the motion: "the probe drifts slowly left, the camera pushes in"> for the
   opening, illustrated sentences that run long, and anything that moves (launches, flybys, falling, light travelling);
   flow_image:<the same kind of description> for short in-between shots. A flow_video shot lasts UNDER 6 SECONDS: when the sentence runs
   longer, write two flow_video shots of the same subject with different framings (wide, then close), the second starting at a pause. Describe ONLY what is visible: place, objects, light,
   weather, camera distance. Same craft = same description every time. No text, no labels, no people's faces.
7. Death, silence, the end of a signal -> add a layer row type fade (the program also detects "went silent", "blacked out").
   kind black is ONLY a short silence: a breath of 2-3 seconds (a signal lost, cameras switched off), never a sentence's picture. The
   next shot row starts on the next sentence; the program cuts any longer black to 2.8 s and gives the rest to a picture.
Never use a filler picture that does not show what the sentence says.
A plate is ONLY the dark ground a diagram is drawn on: every kind plate shot MUST have a diagram (or a title) layer row under it, drawn for
most of the shot. A plate with only a caption or a number is an empty screen: give that sentence a picture (kind image/video) instead,
with the caption or number as a layer on top. For a distance, use diagram id logaxis (e.g. text MOON=1.3 light-seconds; YOU=400,000 km).
