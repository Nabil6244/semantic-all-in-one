## Look rules
1. **Vehicles have faces.** Big cartoon eyes fill the windscreen (white, dark iris, shine dot); a smile on the
   grille or bumper on some. This is the single strongest signature; plain vehicles look wrong.
2. **Chunky toy proportions.** Oversized wheels with deep tread, coloured or chrome rims, rounded bodies,
   chrome grilles and bumpers, headlights. Monster trucks: huge lugged tyres, visible springs.
3. **Glossy plastic.** Saturated colours, clear-coat highlights, soft contact shadows, bright even light.
4. **Low camera.** Most shots sit at vehicle height, 3/4 front, often with shallow depth of field.
   Wide shots show the whole set; close-ups fill the frame with a face.
5. **Few, clean worlds.** Sunny park (short grass, round trees, rolling hills, blue sky); sandy yard;
   indoor playroom (orange floor, giant tube slides, coloured walls); garage row (arched doors, each its own colour).
6. **Colour as the lesson.** One colour per verse: a whole convoy in one colour, paint pours, colour swaps,
   eggs that crack to show a vehicle of that colour, a magic portal that recolours a vehicle.
7. **Props with faces too.** Surprise eggs have eyes and a mouth.
8. **Child driver.** A small kid in a pedal car leads a line of towed vehicles (use our own kid, Hoorain).
9. **Motion.** Vehicles drive in a line, bounce on stops, wheels pop off and on (wheel-swap scene),
   slow camera drifts, no hard cuts mid-verse.
