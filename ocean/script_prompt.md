You are writing the narration for a 9-11 minute YouTube video about ocean animals, in the style of the
"apex predator + what it fears" explainers. Write ORIGINAL words and check every fact; do not copy any
existing video. Topic: [YOUR TOPIC HERE, e.g. "The only animals that make sperm whales dive"]

LENGTH AND VOICE
- 1,550-1,750 words (about 10 minutes at 160-170 words a minute).
- One calm, curious narrator talking to one viewer. Short sentences, mostly 8-20 words.
  A line that lands is often 3-6 words ("They didn't die. They ran.").
- Light dry humour: about one joke comparison a minute ("That's like saying mice keep cats away.").
- No headings, no stage directions, no emojis, no lists in the output: only the words the narrator says,
  one paragraph per beat.

STRUCTURE (follow it in this order)
1. Cold open, second person (0:00-0:40). Start with "Picture this." then "You're ..." placing the viewer
   in the water next to the hero animal. 3-5 punchy facts build the hero up (size, teeth, speed, "nothing hunts it").
2. The turn. One sentence starting with "Except" followed by one shocking specific: a year, a count,
   a place ("In 2009, two orcas arrived at the Farallon Islands. Within hours, every great white fled.").
3. The promise (by 0:60). "So today we're finding out ..." - what the video will answer.
4. Myth bust. "When most people think about X, they bring up A, B, or maybe C. And yes, ... But ..."
   Knock down the obvious answers and set the rule for the list.
5. The ranked list: 4-6 chapters, each ONE animal (or one depth zone), escalating from the most
   obvious/awkward to the scariest. Open each with an ordinal line: "The first animal on our list is the ...",
   "Number two is ...", "And the last one is ...". In each chapter:
   - a named study, place or year ("A 2019 study off South Africa ...", "Researchers tracked 17 sharks ...")
   - at least one hard number with its unit (16 metres, 300 teeth, 4 hours, 90 percent, 1,000 metres down)
   - the mechanism: HOW the animal wins, step by step, with action verbs (rams, circles, dives, flees, bites)
   - one joke comparison.
6. "What would you do?" chapter: back to second person. "You're diving off ... and you see ..."
   Give the viewer real choices as a question ("Would you swim away, or stay still?") and explain the right one.
7. Big-picture close: humans, climate, or "why we're seeing more of this now"; end on a question
   ("So which animal surprised you most? Let us know in the comments.").

WRITE FOR THE PICTURE (the video draws exactly what you say, when you say it)
