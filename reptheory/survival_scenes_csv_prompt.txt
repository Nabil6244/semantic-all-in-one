You are the director of a Survival video: a long-form survival and preparedness explainer drawn as ink-and-watercolour field-guide pictures on white paper (frontier, homestead and modern family life), with code-drawn cards that act out the narration. Turn the SCRIPT at the end into a scenes CSV that tells the app exactly what to show on every sentence. Output the CSV only, in one code block, nothing before or after it.

THE LOOK WE ARE MAKING (read this first)
- A new shot every sentence or two (about every 3-4 seconds). Never a screen of words alone: every sentence sits on a picture or a card.
- About two thirds of the video is drawn pictures (one picture per moment of the story; the same picture again at a closer framing when the narration stays on it), one third is cards that act out what is said (numbers, steps, versus, myth vs fact, the one rule, questions).
- The first shot is the strongest picture of the video's promise. The last shot calls back to it.
- Words on screen are short (1-6 words) and always the narrator's own words.
- There is no 3D and no fixed main character. When the script follows one person (an old-timer, a long hunter, a family), describe them the same way every time.

HOW IT WORKS
One row per sentence of the script, in order. The app lines the rows up with the voiceover by their words, so do NOT write start or end times.
If the script starts with a "TITLE: ..." line, leave that line out: it is not spoken.
The text column must be the script's own words, unchanged: never reword, add, drop, split differently or merge anything.
Never invent a number, a study, a year or a name: the video shows only what the narration says.
The other columns are your direction. A blank cell leaves that choice to the app.

HEADER (exactly this, in this order)
text,chapter,look,prompt,muscle,camera,on_screen,section,graphic

Put every cell that contains a comma in double quotes. Leave a cell empty when it does not apply (keep the commas). Every row has exactly 9 cells. The muscle column is always empty for Survival.

COLUMNS
- text: one script sentence, word for word, punctuation included.
- chapter: a short section name on the FIRST row of each section only, empty on the other rows. It becomes the label at the top of the screen. In a countdown or ranking it is the number and the item: "Number 22 · The blaze", "#50 · Hawaii". Otherwise 2 to 4 words: "The tarp trap", "Hour 1", "The $20 trick".
- look: what this sentence is shown as (list below), or empty to let the app choose.
- prompt: for a picture (look flow), one sentence describing exactly what to draw (rules below). Write "same" to show the previous row's picture again with a new framing (use the camera column).
- muscle: always empty.
- camera: for a picture only: wide, medium or close, optionally with where to look: left, right, top, bottom. Empty = wide for a new picture, medium for a "same" picture. Use close for the detail the sentence is about (the cut in the bark, the knot, the flame, the label on a can); never on two rows in a row.
- on_screen: 1 to 6 words to show big on this sentence, taken from the sentence itself (you may join them with = ≠ + →, and write a spoken number as digits: "#22", "$20", "97%"). Examples: "#22", "BLAZE BOTH SIDES", "COTTON KILLS", "WOOL ≠ COTTON". Every word must be in the sentence or the app ignores the cell.
- section: on the first row of each part: hook, why (how it works, the science, the proof), unit (one item, trick, place or hour), warning (the one rule, a danger and what to do), plan (homework, what to do tonight), result (the payoff of the teased ending, the close).
- graphic: leave empty unless the script announces a numbered set of things by name; then, on the row that announces them: "chain: headlamp | knife | fire steel | pot" with the script's own names.

LOOKS (use a card look ONLY when the sentence has its shape; never force one)
  flow = a drawn picture made for this sentence (describe it in prompt). The default for any sentence about people, an action, an object, a place, a moment in history or a mental picture.
  stat = a stated number in a ring ("about 97% of the heat", "98 degrees", "3 days").
  bars = two or more stated numbers of one unit ("$120 versus $4"), or a stated share ("9 in 10 people").
  slots = a spoken count fills slots one by one: "Here are three of their tricks.", "Seven tools that matter."
  steps = a spoken sequence ("first ... then ... finally", "soak ..., drain ..., rinse ...").
  checklist = a spoken list of three or more short items ("glass, metal, varnish and wool").
  versus = two things compared ("a flashlight versus a headlamp", "a bare oak versus a thick pine").
  myth = "Most people think X. In fact Y." / a belief knocked down.
  mistake = "the mistake almost everybody makes is ..." (a WRONG panel, and a RIGHT panel when the next sentence fixes it).
  warning = the one rule when it names a danger and what to do ("never run a generator indoors; put it twenty feet from the house").
  formula = "X is not Y", "X means Y", "X is like Y" ("Cover only works if it stays at the temperature of its surroundings.").
  timeline = what happens at two or more time marks ("by the first night ... by day three ...").
  study = a study or test with its count or year, only when the script names it.
  promise = only "I'll show you ..." / "at the end I'll tell you ..." lines. A tease of the ending ("And number one is ...", "Remember that thing ...") is a flow row: the app adds the #1 lock on its own words.
  question = a question to the viewer, including the forced-choice question at the end.
  engage = the subscribe tease and the subscribe joke as their screen.
  takeaway = the sentence itself as big words: only for the single final line, if at all.

WRITING A PICTURE PROMPT (the prompt column)
The app adds the art style, the white paper, the colours and the composition (subject on one side, empty space for words). You describe only WHAT is in the picture:
1. Who: give each person a short look once and repeat it word for word every time they appear ("a lean long hunter in buckskins and a fur cap", "an 81-year-old farmer in a flannel shirt and a worn cap", "a mother, a father and two young kids in winter coats"). Historical people wear their period's clothes; modern families wear ordinary clothes.
2. Doing what, exactly: the action at this moment, mid-motion ("cutting a pale blaze into pine bark at eye level with a hatchet, chips flying").
3. With what and where: every object and place the sentence names, and the season and time of day when the sentence gives them (snow, dusk, a dark street, a cellar).
4. The detail the sentence is about drawn large and clear: the cut, the knot, the flame, the gap of air under the blanket, the frost on the window.
5. Seen from where: side view, front view, three-quarter view, from above (for drones, maps of a yard, a camp layout), close-up on hands or an object.
6. One moment per picture, no comic panels, no split screens, no text, letters, numbers, logos, brand names or arrows in the picture (the app draws them).
7. Keep it calm and safe: no injuries, blood, bodies, guns pointed at people or fighting; show the danger as a scene (a dark house in a storm, a car in snow, a drone in the night sky) and the people doing the right thing.
8. Mental pictures get their own picture: "a person-shaped light bulb" is a person glowing softly in a dark field; "a dark mirror" is a window reflecting the yard.
9. Never draw what the sentence denies: "a bush won't hide you" is NOT a well-hidden person; it is a person in a bush, plainly visible.
10. Two contrasting sentences get two DIFFERENT prompts that show the difference. Never copy a prompt to the next row; write "same" only for the very same picture again.
11. Keep each row to one clause of at most ~6 seconds of speech.

RHYTHM RULES
1. Row 1 is always the hook picture: look flow, one image of the video's promise. Never a card on row 1.
2. In a countdown or ranking, the row that says the number ("That's number 22.") gets the chapter "Number 22 · <item>", on_screen "#22", section unit, and a flow picture of the item.
3. Direct about half the rows (the hook, each new item, every number, comparison, rule, step list, the teased ending, every question and call to action and the last line); leave the others blank.
4. Never more than 3 card rows in a row; never more than 4 flow rows in a row; never the same card look on two rows in a row.
5. Every one rule is a warning, mistake or formula row (whichever shape it has); every belief knocked down is a myth row; every tease or reminder of the ending is a flow row (the app shows the #1 lock on it).
6. Every subscribe line is an engage row; the forced-choice question is a question row.
7. The last row calls back to row 1: look flow, row 1's prompt written out again, camera close, plus an on_screen phrase from the last sentence.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
- Joined together, the text column is the script, word for word (without the TITLE line).
- Row 1 is a flow hook picture; the last row is the callback picture.
- No card look on a sentence that lacks its shape.
- Every on_screen word appears in its own sentence (a spoken number may be written as digits).
- Every flow row has a prompt (or "same"); every person is described the same way each time.
- 9 cells per row, commas inside cells are quoted.

Of course, a marked trail only helps if you can see where the sun is.
