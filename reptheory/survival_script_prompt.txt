You are writing the voiceover script for a Survival video: a long-form survival and preparedness explainer (20 to 45 minutes) for ordinary families, drawn as ink-and-watercolour field-guide pictures on white paper. Write the script for the TOPIC at the end. Output the title line first, then the script only, as plain sentences, nothing else.

WHO IS LISTENING
An ordinary adult with a family, a house, a car and a modest budget, who worries about blackouts, winter storms, shortages and worse. Not a soldier, not a rich prepper. They want to know what actually works, what is a waste of money, and what they can do tonight. Talk to them as "you", warmly and plainly, like a calm neighbour who has lived through a few hard winters.

STEP 1: PICK THE FORMAT THAT FITS THE TOPIC
1. COUNTDOWN: "25 forgotten frontier tricks", "15 skills you can learn in one day". Items counted down from the highest number to number one.
2. ONE SECRET, MANY MYTHS: "Hide from thermal drones with a $20 trick". One promised answer held to the end; the chapters knock down the wrong answers on the way.
3. PLACE RANKING: "If the grid collapses, these states go dark first". Places ranked from worst to best (or first to last), each with its weak spot and the fix.
4. TIMELINE: "The first 7 days of a total blackout, hour by hour". The story moves forward in time; each step says what goes wrong and what to do.
5. PERSONA STORY: "9 items old-timers buy every winter". One believable person (a first name, an age, a place, the hard times they lived through) carries every item. Make the person up and keep them modest and believable; never present them as a real, named public figure.
6. THEY LIED (GEAR): "They lied about survival gear: the only 7 tools that matter". The version they sell you against the version that works, with a simple test that sorts them.

STEP 2: THE TITLE (first line, starting with "TITLE: ")
Mix two of these: a number + the thing + the disaster ("15 Ways to Purify WATER After SHTF"); a they-lied or insider line ("They Lied About...", "Nobody Has the Guts to Tell You"); old wisdom ("Old-Timers", "Depression Grandmas", "Frontier", "Before Electricity"); a pointed number ("Almost Everyone Gets #4 Wrong"); a price ("a $20 Trick", "All Under $2"). Put one to three words in CAPITALS. Two parts joined by an em dash work well. Never promise something the script does not deliver.

STEP 3: THE OPENING (the first 30 to 45 seconds)
1. Open on a concrete scene or a claim that flips a belief. Examples of the shape: "That 40-pound bag in your closet was packed by somebody who wanted your money." / "Most emergency advice tells you to be ready for 3 days. On the frontier, help could be a whole season away."
2. Give the whole video ONE LENS in a sentence or two: a simple test or idea every item is judged by ("the verb test": a tool must give your hands a job they can't do bare; "think in layers"; "every state lives on what it already has").
3. TEASE THE ENDING with a strange, specific detail that makes no sense yet ("And number one is a sickness that shows up around the third month in forts stocked with food for the whole winter."). In format 2 this is the promise ("a way to hide for about $20, and it starts with something already in a lot of first aid kits"), followed by a twist ("most people who own one would use it in the exact way that gets them spotted").
4. In a countdown, tease one middle item with a cost ("Number seven costs $300 if you get it wrong, and you won't know until you're already sick.").
5. No subscribe request, no channel name, no long disclaimer in the opening.

STEP 4: EVERY ITEM / CHAPTER (60 to 120 seconds each)
Write each one in this order. Leave out a part the item really doesn't have, but never leave out the hook, the rule or the bridge.
1. HOOK before the number: a line that makes them curious ("We still say somebody blazed a trail, and almost nobody remembers what a blaze actually is.") or a second-person scene ("Night one on the walk home. Try fixing anything in the dark with a flashlight in your mouth.").
2. THE NUMBER, said plainly on its own: "That's number 22." / "Number 15 works like this." (countdown and ranking formats).
3. THE STORY: who did this and when (trail diaries, long hunters, Depression kitchens, an old-timer's barn, a real recent event), with one human detail and one small joke if it fits.
4. WHY IT WORKS: simple science with one memorable number ("water pulls heat from your body about 25 times faster than air").
5. HOW TO DO IT TODAY: two to four short steps in order, with real amounts, sizes and times ("soak a cup of dry lentils overnight", "every finger is about 15 minutes of daylight").
6. THE ONE RULE: a single safety rule or mistake, said as a rule ("The one rule that matters: only dung from animals that eat grass."). Water, food, medicine, fire, carbon monoxide, cold and heat ALWAYS get their real safety rule.
7. THE PUNCHLINE: one dry, quotable line ("It was the original pellet stove, and the bison delivered it for free.").
8. THE BRIDGE: the last sentence opens the next item ("Of course, all that travel only helps if you can find your way home again.").
Say what it costs, how long it takes and how hard it is whenever the item has those ("about $2", "takes three days", "a child can do it"): the app shows them under the item's title.

STEP 5: KEEP THEM WATCHING
1. Every 5 or 6 minutes, point back to the teased ending ("Remember that thing sitting in a lot of first aid kits? Hold on to it.").
2. Two or three times, point forward to a later item ("keep this in mind, it matters again in number 8") and call back to an earlier one ("the same salt from number 19").
3. Put a vivid mental picture in every 20 to 30 seconds ("a person-shaped light bulb", "a dark mirror", "a lattice of bare branches like a chain-link fence"): each one becomes a picture on screen.
4. Keep the family in it: the kids, the elderly parent, the dog, the neighbours.
5. Once, about two thirds in, use subscribe as a tease, not a request: "If you just pictured your own roof on that screen, hit subscribe, because next is the hour you glow the brightest."
6. Once, be honest about the limits ("I'll be honest, nothing makes you invisible for hours."). It makes everything else believable.

STEP 6: THE ENDING (the last 60 to 90 seconds)
1. Pay off the teased ending in full, with its two common wrong ways and the right way.
2. Bring back the lens in one line.
3. HOMEWORK TONIGHT: one small, real thing to do this week ("dump your bag out on the floor", "run one evening with the main breaker off while help is still a phone call away").
4. A FORCED-CHOICE QUESTION for the comments: "You can only carry four of the seven. Which three stay home?" / "Would you rather ... or ...? Pick a side and say which state you're in."
5. A SUBSCRIBE JOKE tied to the topic ("Frontier families never had a subscribe button, and most people who watch to the end still skip it."), then the NEXT VIDEO tease in one sentence.
6. The very last line calls back to the first line or the lens, short.

WRITING RULES
- Plain spoken English, short sentences, one idea per sentence. Write numbers as you would say them in a sentence; years as digits.
- Never invent a study, a statistic, a date, a quote or a historical event. If you are not sure, say it generally ("trail diaries are full of jokes about it") or leave it out.
- No medical, legal or weapons instructions beyond basic, widely published safety advice. No advice to break the law. Treat looters, strangers and conflict as things to avoid, never to attack.
- No fear for its own sake: every danger comes with what to do about it.
- Length: about 150 words per minute of video. A 30-minute video is about 4,500 words; a 40-minute countdown of 25 items is about 6,000.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
- The first line is "TITLE: ..." and the title's promise is delivered.
- The opening has the scene or flipped belief, the lens, the tease of the ending, and no subscribe request.
- Every item has a hook, a rule and a bridge; the number is said on its own sentence in countdowns and rankings.
- The teased ending is pointed back to at least twice and paid off at the end.
- There is one honesty line, one subscribe tease, homework, a forced-choice question, a subscribe joke, a next-video tease and a callback last line.
- No invented facts.
