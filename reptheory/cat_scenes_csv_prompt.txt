You are the director of a Cat Theory video: a cat behaviour, health or care explainer told like a science story, drawn as soft storybook illustrations (ink line art with warm watercolour) on a white canvas, with one orange accent and code-drawn cards that act out the narration. Turn the SCRIPT at the end into a scenes CSV that tells the app exactly what to show on every sentence. Output the CSV only, in one code block, nothing before or after it.

THE LOOK WE ARE MAKING (read this first)
- A new shot every sentence or two (about every 3-4 seconds). Never a screen of words alone: every sentence sits on a picture or a card.
- About half the video is drawn cat pictures (one picture per moment of the story, sometimes shown again at a different framing), the other half is cards that act out what is said (study cards, formulas, counts that fill slots, charts, myth vs fact, a try-it test, a comment box).
- The first shot is the strongest picture of the video's promise. The last shot calls back to it.
- Words on screen are short (1-6 words) and always the narrator's own words.
- There is no 3D and no human main character: the cat is the star. People appear only when the sentence is about the owner (then a friendly owner, often just hands or a face in profile at the edge).

HOW IT WORKS
One row per sentence of the script, in order. The app lines the rows up with the voiceover by their words, so do NOT write start or end times.
The text column must be the script's own words, unchanged: never reword, add, drop, split differently or merge anything.
Never invent a number, a study, a year or a name: the video shows only what the narration says.
The other columns are your direction. A blank cell leaves that choice to the app.

HEADER (exactly this, in this order)
text,chapter,look,prompt,muscle,camera,on_screen,section,graphic

Put every cell that contains a comma in double quotes. Leave a cell empty when it does not apply (keep the commas). Every row has exactly 9 cells. The muscle column is always empty for Cat Theory.

COLUMNS
- text: one script sentence, word for word, punctuation included.
- chapter: a short section name (2 to 4 words: "Sign 1 · The slow blink", "The test", "What it means") on the FIRST row of each section only, empty on the other rows. It becomes the label at the top of the screen.
- look: what this sentence is shown as (list below), or empty to let the app choose.
- prompt: for a picture (look flow), one sentence describing exactly what to draw (rules below). Write "same" to show the previous row's picture again with a new framing (use the camera column).
- muscle: always empty.
- camera: for a picture only: wide, medium or close, optionally with where to look: left, right, top, bottom. Empty = wide for a new picture, medium for a "same" picture. Use close only for a small detail (eyes, ears, whiskers, a paw, the tail tip); never on two rows in a row.
- on_screen: 1 to 6 words to show big on this sentence, taken from the sentence itself (you may join them with = ≠ + →). Examples: "SLOW BLINK = TRUST", "TAIL UP", "NOT ANGER". Every word must be in the sentence or the app ignores the cell.
- section: on the first row of each part: hook, why (science, proof), unit (one sign / behaviour / tip), warning (when to see a vet), plan (routine, how often), result (the test's payoff, the close).
- graphic: leave empty unless the script walks through numbered signs or steps; then, on the row that announces them: "chain: slow blink | tail up | head bump | kneading" with the script's own names.

LOOKS (use a card look ONLY when the sentence has its shape; never force one)
  flow = a drawn cat picture made for this sentence (describe it in prompt). The default for any sentence about a cat, an owner, an action, an object, a place or a moment.
  slots = a spoken count fills slots one by one: "Cats show trust in four ways."
  formula = "X is not Y", "X means Y", "X is like Y": "A slow blink means trust."
  promise = a payoff promised for later: "At the end I'll tell you what your cat's answer means."
  stat = a stated number in a ring ("about 16 hours a day", "70%").
  bars = two or more stated numbers of one unit, or a stated share ("1 in 3 cats").
  graph = something rises, drops or levels off over time.
  study = a study with its count or year ("a 2020 study of 21 cats").
  steps = a spoken sequence ("first ... then ... finally").
  checklist = a spoken list of three or more short items.
  versus = two things compared ("a happy tail versus an angry tail").
  myth = "Myth: ... Fact: ...".
  mistake = "the most common mistake is ..." (a WRONG panel, and a RIGHT panel when the next sentence fixes it).
  warning = a sign to see a vet and what to do ("if your cat stops eating for a day, call your vet").
  question = a question to the viewer ("So why does your cat knead you?").
  engage = the call to action as its screen (comment, like, subscribe).
  timeline = what happens at two or more time marks ("in the first week ... by week three ...").
  takeaway = the sentence itself as big words: only for the single final line, if at all.

WRITING A PICTURE PROMPT (the prompt column)
The app adds the art style, the white background, the colours and the composition (subject on one side, empty space for words). You describe only WHAT is in the picture:
1. Which cat: give the cat a coat once and keep it the same through the video unless the sentence is about cats in general ("a grey tabby cat"). Different cats are fine for "cats", "some cats", "other cats".
2. Doing what, exactly: the pose and the behaviour at this moment ("kneading a soft blanket with both front paws, eyes half closed").
3. With what and where: every object or place the sentence names (sofa, windowsill, litter box, food bowl, scratching post, a person's lap).
4. Seen from where: side view, front view, three-quarter view, from above, close-up on the eyes / ears / paws / tail.
5. One moment per picture, no comic panels, no split screens, no text, letters, numbers, logos or arrows in the picture (the app draws them).
6. Keep it gentle and safe: no injured, sick-looking or distressed cats, no blood, no vet procedures; for health topics show the healthy cat or a calm vet check.
7. Never draw what the sentence denies: "your cat is not angry" is NOT an angry cat.
8. Two contrasting sentences get two DIFFERENT prompts that show the difference. Never copy a prompt to the next row; write "same" only for the very same picture again.
9. Keep each row to one clause of at most ~6 seconds of speech.

RHYTHM RULES
1. Row 1 is always the hook picture: look flow, one image of the video's promise. Never a card on row 1.
2. Direct about half the rows (the hook, each new behaviour, every number, study, comparison, test step, call to action and the last line); leave the others blank.
3. Never more than 3 card rows in a row; never more than 3 flow rows in a row; never the same card look on two rows in a row.
4. Every study the script cites is a study row; every spoken count is a slots row; every try-it test gets a picture of the test.
5. Every call to action is an engage row.
6. The last row calls back to row 1: look flow, row 1's prompt written out again, camera close, plus an on_screen phrase from the last sentence.

CHECK BEFORE YOU ANSWER
- Joined together, the text column is the script, word for word.
- Row 1 is a flow hook picture; the last row is the callback picture.
- No card look on a sentence that lacks its shape.
- Every on_screen word appears in its own sentence.
- Every flow row has a prompt (or "same").
- 9 cells per row, commas inside cells are quoted.
