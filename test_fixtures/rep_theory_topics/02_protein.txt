Title: How Much Protein Do You Really Need?
Everyone tells you to eat more protein, but nobody tells you how much. Too little and your training is wasted. Too much and you're just paying for expensive food. Let's find your number.
The research is clear. If you train, aim for about one point six grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. Above that, a big review of forty-nine studies found almost no extra muscle.
So a seventy kilogram person needs around one hundred and twelve grams a day. That sounds like a lot, so split it up.
Eat three or four meals with twenty five to forty grams each. Your body uses protein better when it comes in steady portions instead of one giant dinner.
Here are easy sources. Three eggs give you about eighteen grams. A chicken breast gives you around thirty. A cup of Greek yogurt gives you twenty. A can of tuna gives you twenty five.
Plant protein works too. Lentils, tofu and beans all count, you just need a little more of them.
The most common mistake is eating almost no protein at breakfast and trying to make it all up at night.
Another myth: protein damages your kidneys. In healthy people, studies have not found that. If you already have kidney disease, talk to your doctor first.
And protein shakes are not magic. They are just food in a bottle. Use them when real food is hard.
Do you hit your number? Count it for one day and comment how close you got.
