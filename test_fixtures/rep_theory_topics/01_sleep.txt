Title: Why Sleep Builds More Muscle Than Your Workout
You train hard, you eat well, and you still look the same. The missing piece is probably your sleep. Your muscles don't grow in the gym. They grow at night, while you sleep. Here is what happens, and three changes you can make tonight.
First, the numbers. Most adults need seven to nine hours. In one study, people who slept five and a half hours lost sixty percent more muscle while dieting than people who slept eight and a half. Same food, same training, very different bodies.
During deep sleep your body releases most of its growth hormone. Your brain clears out waste, and your nervous system resets so your next workout feels stronger.
Change number one: keep the same wake-up time every day, even on weekends. Your body clock likes routine, and a steady wake-up time makes falling asleep easier.
Change number two: cool the room down. Around eighteen degrees is ideal. A cool room helps your core temperature drop, and that drop is the signal to sleep.
Change number three: no screens for thirty minutes before bed. Bright light tells your brain it is still daytime.
The most common mistake is trying to catch up on the weekend. Sleeping in on Sunday shifts your body clock, and Monday night gets even worse.
What about naps? A short nap of twenty minutes helps. A two-hour nap can steal your night.
And caffeine stays in your body for hours. A coffee at four in the afternoon is still half there at ten at night.
Try it for two weeks. Write down your hours and how your workouts feel. Comment your average sleep below.
