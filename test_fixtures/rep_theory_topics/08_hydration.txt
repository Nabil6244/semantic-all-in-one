Title: Water and Your Workout
Losing just two percent of your body weight in water can make your workouts noticeably worse. For a seventy kilogram person, that is only one point four kilograms of sweat.
You feel it as heavy legs, a higher heart rate and a workout that feels harder than it should.
So how much should you drink? A simple guide is to drink enough that your urine is pale yellow. Dark yellow means drink more. Completely clear all day can mean you're overdoing it.
Before training, drink a glass of water about two hours before. During training, take small sips every fifteen to twenty minutes.
After training, weigh yourself. For every kilogram you lost, drink about one and a half liters over the next few hours.
If you sweat a lot or train for over an hour, add salt. Sodium helps your body hold on to the water instead of passing it straight through.
The most common mistake is drinking a huge amount at once. Your body can only absorb so much at a time, and the rest is lost.
And yes, coffee and tea count toward your water. The idea that coffee dehydrates you is mostly a myth at normal amounts.
Track your water for three days and comment what you found.
