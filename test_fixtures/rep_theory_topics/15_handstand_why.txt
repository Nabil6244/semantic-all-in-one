Title: Why Your Handstand Keeps Falling Over
A handstand isn't a strength trick. It's a balance problem, and I'll prove it.
Your body is a lever, and the load on your wrists is weight times distance.
Lean too far forward and you tip over. Lean back and you fall onto your feet.
A good handstand is half strength, half balance.
Your shoulders get stronger faster than your wrists, so the wrists set the pace.
When the wrists hurt, your body says stop. Careful here, a wrist strain can take weeks to heal.
Some people get a free handstand in a few months, others take one to two years, some never.
Training drains your wrists and rest rebuilds them, so rest at least two days a week.
Start with a plank, then a push-up, then a wall walk, and only then kick up.
This drill is stupid easy, trust me.
Most people who can hold one are around 170 cm and 70 kg, but height matters less than practice.
The wall turns a scary fall into a safe lesson.
Your plan: wrist warm-up, two wall holds, three kick-ups, plank. 4 days a week, 15 minutes.
This is the road to your first free handstand.
Now the two questions you ask most.
Question one: do I need a wall? Yes, for the first month it keeps you safe.
You asked: will it hurt my wrists? Not if you warm them up and build up slowly.
Comment how many seconds you can hold today.
