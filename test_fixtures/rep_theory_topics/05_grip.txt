Title: Why Your Grip Strength Predicts How Long You Live
Your grip strength says more about your health than almost any number in the gym. A study of one hundred and forty thousand adults found that weaker grip was linked to higher risk of heart disease and early death.
That doesn't mean squeezing harder makes you live longer. It means grip is a window into your whole body's strength.
And it is easy to train. Here are three exercises.
Exercise one is the dead hang. Hang from a bar with straight arms for as long as you can. Aim for sixty seconds. Your forearms, your fingers and your shoulders all work.
Exercise two is the farmer's carry. Hold something heavy in each hand and walk for forty steps. Shoulders back, don't let your wrists bend.
Exercise three is the towel squeeze. Wring out a wet towel as hard as you can, ten times in each direction.
Do them twice a week, at the end of your workout.
The mistake most people make is using straps for everything. Straps help you lift more, but your grip never gets the chance to catch up.
How strong should your grip be? Many adult men squeeze around forty kilograms on a hand dynamometer, many women around twenty five.
Your grip also gets better fast. Most people improve within four weeks.
Test your dead hang today and comment your time.
