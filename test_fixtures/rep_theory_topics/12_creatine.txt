Title: Creatine: Myths vs Facts
Creatine is the most studied sports supplement in the world, and still one of the most misunderstood. Let's go through the myths.
Myth one: creatine is a steroid. It is not. Your body makes creatine every day, and you get it from meat and fish. The supplement just tops up your stores.
Myth two: it damages your kidneys. Dozens of studies in healthy people taking normal doses for up to five years found no harm. If you have kidney problems, ask your doctor.
Myth three: it makes you bloated and fat. Creatine pulls water into your muscles, not under your skin. You may gain one or two kilograms in the first weeks, and that is water inside the muscle.
Myth four: you need to load it. You can take twenty grams a day for a week to fill up faster, but three to five grams a day reaches the same level in about a month.
So what does it actually do? It helps you squeeze out an extra rep or two on hard sets. Over months, those extra reps add up to more strength and muscle.
New research also suggests it may help your brain, especially when you are short on sleep.
The mistake is buying expensive blends. Plain creatine monohydrate is the form with the most evidence, and it is cheap.
