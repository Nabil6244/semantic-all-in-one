On the first of March 1982, a Soviet lander called Venera 13 touched down on Venus. Two hours and seven minutes later, it went silent.

The ground is 464 degrees Celsius, hot enough to melt lead. And the air presses down about 90 times harder than on Earth. It's also 96.5 percent carbon dioxide, with no oxygen at all. Unprotected, you'd last only seconds.

But 55 kilometres up, in the clouds, it's 27 degrees, like a summer day. Drop to 48 kilometres, and the acid clouds end. At 30 kilometres up, it's already 222 degrees.

Venera 13 sent home the first colour photos from the surface of another planet.

Every one of those pictures, and every reading from its instruments, crossed the gap between the two worlds as radio waves travelling at the speed of light, a journey of a few minutes that no human has ever made in person.
