At exactly eleven A.M. Greenwich Mean Time on November eighteenth, two thousand twenty-six, history happened.
The global review embargo for Grand Theft Auto Six lifted simultaneously with New Zealand’s midnight launch.
Within minutes, one hundred and nine critic reviews pushed the game to a ninety-nine on Metacritic.
That score tied Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game in human history.
It beat Red Dead Redemption Two, Breath of the Wild, and Super Mario Odyssey at ninety-seven.
Over nine million players had already pre-ordered the game, generating one point five billion dollars.
PlayStation Five console sales had even jumped twenty-two percent in a single month just for tonight.
Millions of players spent the week preloading two hundred and twelve gigabytes onto their console drives.
Thousands more tried changing their console locations to Wellington just to play fifteen hours early.
New Zealand’s official tourism board even posted memes celebrating their status as global time travelers.
Meanwhile, Xbox warned reporters against region switching and Twitch threatened bans for faking New Zealand residency.
Even PlayStation players faced account ban warnings for registering fake secondary accounts in Auckland.
Yet while the internet celebrated that historic ninety-nine rating, early players uncovered a massive shock.
Senior Editor Simon Cardy at IGN published an urgent warning less than an hour into launch.
Players finishing the opening Prologue noticed something bizarre inside the Grand Theft Auto Six pause menu.
A staple feature that existed in every Rockstar game for seventeen straight years had vanished.
Worse yet, players who paid one hundred dollars for the Ultimate Edition discovered a brutal catch.
Advancing through the main campaign can permanently strip your paid bonus items away from your character.
And if you overwrite a single autosave file across the fifty-hour story, there is no undo button.
You can permanently lock yourself out of entire chapters, safehouses, and endings before the credits roll.
To understand why your save files are suddenly in danger, look back to two thousand nine.
When Rockstar released The Ballad of Gay Tony, they introduced a beloved feature called Mission Replay.
Red Dead Redemption, Grand Theft Auto Five, and Red Dead Redemption Two all expanded that exact system.
In those games, finishing a heist or shootout unlocked a checklist of bronze, silver, and gold medals.
You could open your pause menu anytime, select any past mission, and jump right back in.
If you wanted to replay a bank robbery with a different approach, it took two clicks.
When players booted up Grand Theft Auto Six today and finished the Prologue, they checked the menu.
First, players noticed that the Prologue mission contained zero Gold Medal objectives or completion checklists.
Then they opened the completed missions database inside the pause screen expecting to see a replay prompt.
Instead, the entire completed missions archive is nothing more than a wall of static thumbnail images.
Clicking on a finished mission does nothing at all because Rockstar removed Mission Replay at launch.
You cannot replay individual story missions, and you cannot select previous story chapters from any menu.
Even in their ten-out-of-ten masterpiece review, IGN called this omission a baffling step backward for Rockstar.
Once a mission ends and the autosave icon spins in the corner, that moment is gone.
If you missed a branching path during a heist, you cannot reload that mission from the menu.
Why would Rockstar spend ten years building the most advanced open world ever and cut Mission Replay?
The answer is tied to how Leonida tracks your bank account, your safehouses, and your fugitive status.
And that exact same fugitive system is now blindsiding everyone who bought the hundred-dollar Ultimate Edition.
Back on June twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six, Rockstar officially opened digital pre-orders across PlayStation and Xbox.
The Standard Edition launched at seventy-nine ninety-nine, while the digital-only Ultimate Edition cost ninety-nine ninety-nine.
An IGN poll of over twenty-three thousand readers revealed that seventy percent chose the hundred-dollar edition.
That made Grand Theft Auto Six the first true hundred-dollar mainstream release for millions of players.
On paper, the Ultimate Edition promised a massive vault of single-player vehicles, weapons, and cosmetics.
Buyers before November twentieth received the Vintage Vice City Pack alongside Wyman’s Classic Car Collection.
The bundle also listed a ninety-five Grotti Cheetah supercar, Jason’s Safehouse Vehicles, and custom Morgan Revolvers.
Plus, it promised exclusive Vice City Style clothing and tattoos for both Jason and Lucia.
Most players assumed those items would sit waiting inside their wardrobe and garage in Chapter One.
Today, Simon Cardy at IGN revealed three harsh realities about how Ultimate Edition content actually works.
Catch number one is that your exclusive Ultimate Edition clothes and tattoos are not free in-game.
Paying twenty extra real-world dollars only unlocks the right to purchase those cosmetics inside Leonida’s shops.
Even worse, those bonus outfits and tattoos are priced among the most expensive items in the state.
You will not even have enough in-game cash to buy your paid cosmetics until much later.
Catch number two is that Ultimate Edition vehicles, guns, and outfits are strictly locked behind specific chapters.
You cannot drive Wyman’s Classic Car Collection or equip every bonus weapon whenever you feel like it.
And catch number three is the one causing uproar across social media right now.
IGN confirmed that several Ultimate Edition items are permanently taken away from you as the story progresses.
Why would a video game confiscate items that you paid real money to unlock?
Unlike Grand Theft Auto Five, you cannot freely purchase luxury mansions or safehouses across the map.
Players can only buy separate garages and vehicle storage bays on their own terms.
Your actual safehouses shift automatically through the narrative as Jason and Lucia go on the run.
When law enforcement or rival syndicates burn a safehouse chapter down, that chapter’s tied bonuses vanish.
And because there is no chapter select or mission replay, advancing past that point is permanent.
Unless you follow a very specific manual save strategy, your favorite chapter and bonus gear disappear forever.
Here is why the fifteen manual save slots inside Grand Theft Auto Six are your only lifeline.
When you open the Save Game menu tonight, you will find exactly fifteen manual save slots.
According to IGN’s fifty-hour campaign review, the main story takes roughly fifty hours to beat.
If you want the PlayStation Five Platinum Trophy, expect to invest around two hundred hours total.
Because Rockstar deleted Mission Replay, those fifteen manual slots are the only chapter select you get.
IGN’s launch guide urges every player to create a brand-new manual save every five to ten percent.
Instead of overwriting Slot One every night, you must rotate chronologically from Slot One through Slot Fourteen.
Doing that preserves a playable snapshot of every major safehouse, map region, and Ultimate Edition vehicle.
Certain story milestones also act as strict money gates that require accumulating specific cash totals to advance.
Staggering your saves every five to ten percent ensures you never get trapped before a major transition.
But why did we say to rotate through Slot Fourteen instead of using all fifteen slots for chapters?
Because Slot Fifteen needs to be reserved as your dedicated Chaos and Rampage save file.
Here is the hidden behavioral mechanic that makes a separate chaos save mandatory in Leonida.
Both Jason and Lucia have independent Criminal Profiles that evaluate how you commit crimes across the state.
If you rob targets cleanly using zip-ties and intimidation without slaughtering bystanders, your profile stays Professional.
If you go on reckless rampages and gun down civilians, your profile slides toward Psychopath.
And during the Rockstar North hands-on previews, testers noticed a chilling detail beneath the Psychopath tier.
If you push a character’s brutality below Psychopath, the meter hits an irreversible cracked icon state.
Once that icon cracks on your active save file, you can never restore your Professional profile again.
Now connect that irreversible cracked icon to what IGN just confirmed in their launch review today.
IGN confirmed that Grand Theft Auto Six is the first entry built specifically to encourage player empathy.
More importantly, the review confirmed that Grand Theft Auto Six features multiple different story endings.
How you behave across Leonida directly shapes how Jason and Lucia’s story culminates in the finale.
That is why IGN explicitly warned players to keep a separate save file before going on rampages.
And if you want classic chaos without ruining your Criminal Profile, Rockstar hid a reward at the end.
Beating the main story unlocks an exhaustive built-in list of hilarious official cheat codes.
Until you reach those credits, however, one unbacked rampage on your main save can alter your ending forever.
Even if you manage your fifteen save slots carefully, muscle memory from past Rockstar games can betray you.
Remember how Red Dead Redemption Two players constantly shot town sheriffs in the face by mistake?
You would walk up to greet a stranger, tap the wrong trigger, and accidentally draw your revolver.
In a game where civilian murder permanently cracks your Criminal Profile, accidental shootings would be catastrophic.
So for the first time in modern Rockstar history, they completely rebuilt their on-foot controller layout.
You no longer mash the X button on PlayStation or the A button on Xbox to sprint.
Rockstar officially moved Sprint to L3 by clicking the left analog stick, saving thumbs worldwide.
They also separated the weapon draw input so you require a deliberate command before aiming a gun.
That prevents you from accidentally pulling a pistol when you are only trying to talk to NPCs.
Inside vehicles, however, the button changes require an immediate mental adjustment during your first police chase.
On PlayStation Five, the vehicle handbrake has been moved from R1 down to the X button.
Rockstar made that change because L1 and R1 are now dedicated to aiming and firing while driving.
If you tap R1 expecting to drift around a corner in Vice City, you will fire your weapon.
World interactions are also much more physical than anything in Grand Theft Auto Five.
You can no longer stand by the front wheel of a jumbo jet and teleport into the cockpit.
You actually have to find a physical boarding path to climb inside large aircraft at the airport.
Before you even reach the title screen tonight, there are also three mandatory technical hurdles to clear.
First, Sony issued an urgent reminder that PlayStation Five system firmware fourteen point two-two is required.
Without installing firmware fourteen point two-two first, the digital game license will refuse to boot at midnight.
Second, even if you preloaded all two hundred and twelve gigabytes, a mandatory day-one patch awaits.
Update version one point zero-zero-two adds six hundred and two megabytes on PlayStation Five.
On Xbox Series X and S, that same day-one patch requires a one point five-nine gigabyte download.
Third, early technical reviews confirmed that the base PlayStation Five struggles during heavy explosion sequences.
While the game targets thirty frames per second, high-speed chases with heavy explosions trigger noticeable frame drops.
When your countdown timer finally hits zero tonight, follow this exact checklist before starting Chapter One.
Step one: install console firmware fourteen point two-two and patch one point zero-zero-two thirty minutes before midnight.
Step two: the moment you finish the opening Prologue, open your pause menu and create Manual Save One.
Never overwrite that Prologue save so you always have a clean starting point without replaying tutorials.
Step three: every time your story completion climbs by five to ten percent, open a fresh manual slot.
Rotate sequentially through Slots Two to Thirteen whenever you unlock a new safehouse or Ultimate Edition vehicle.
That guarantees that when the story confiscates a safehouse or bonus car, you still own a playable chapter.
Step four: keep Slot Fourteen as your pre-finale checkpoint so you can experience all of the multiple endings.
Step five: reserve Slot Fifteen exclusively for five-star rampages so you never crack your main Criminal Profile.
And finally, remember that L3 sprints and X pulls the handbrake before your first getaway begins.
