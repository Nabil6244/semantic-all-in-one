Tonight you open the GTA 6 map, and almost all of it is pitch black. That is not a glitch. Rockstar is hiding everything you have not explored yet, and what sits under that darkness is about twice the size of the GTA 5 map. There is also a military base Rockstar has never shown anyone, and wild monkeys living somewhere you would never guess. And one cheap shirt could be the difference between getting caught and escaping a six star chase. So before you drive off a bridge on day one, here is what is actually hiding under the black. Six regions, one secret base, real monkeys, the new police system explained, and at the end, the exact route I am taking on my first night. One quick rule before we start. If Rockstar said it themselves, I will tell you it is confirmed. If it comes from people who saw the preview builds, I will say previewers reported it. And if it is just me guessing, I will say so out loud. After going through everything, one pattern jumped out at me, and I will give it to you at the end, because it changes how you should play every single region. First, the size. Rockstar North's Aaron Garbut told Game Informer this is the biggest map the studio has ever built, by a clear margin, roughly double GTA 5. Then Rockstar gave Edge a different comparison, saying Leonida's landmass is close to GTA 4, GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2 added together. Those two comparisons do not match perfectly, and Rockstar has never published a number in kilometers, so any exact figure you see online is a fan estimate. What Edge did say is that a full in-game day lasts much longer than in GTA 5, and the roads are narrower, which makes everything feel packed. And a Rockstar narrative lead admitted there is not enough time to walk you through every corner. Play only the story missions and parts of that map will stay black. So let us light it up, starting where the game starts. The Leonida Keys, Rockstar's version of the Florida Keys, long bridges with water on both sides. Previews say this is where Jason's story begins, and here is a detail I love. His home is not even his. It belongs to Brian Heder, an old school smuggler who runs a boat yard with his third wife, Lori, and he lets Jason stay for free as long as Jason helps with some local work. That is the most Florida rental deal I have ever heard. Now the part the trailers skipped. Previews describe a large military base at the far end of the Keys, and Rockstar has not shown it yet. Remember Fort Zancudo in GTA 5, the first place half of us tried to break into? You know exactly where I am steering my first car. The water here is beautiful, but it is crowded. Game Informer counted more than 170 animal species across land, sea and sky. Flamingos, manatees, pelicans, alligators, and invasive species just like real Florida, green iguanas, cane toads, and rhesus monkeys. Hold on to those monkeys, because we are coming back to them. The rule of the Keys is money. Previews say the cash you carry disappears if you die or get arrested, and reviewers report you can only deposit it at an ATM, not through your phone. Lesson one, find an ATM. Now head northwest, where the concrete roads suddenly stop. This is Grassrivers, Rockstar's take on the Everglades. Rockstar's description says alligators are the famous attraction, but deadlier predators and stranger discoveries are waiting deep in the mangroves. Which predators? They would not say. My guess is Burmese pythons. In the real Everglades they are an invasive species that eats birds, mammals, even alligators. That is my theory, and nothing confirms it. The rule here is how you move. This is airboat country, and an airboat glides over grass where a normal boat gets stuck. On foot, if you cannot see the bottom, do not step in. In open water, Edge says boats react to the waves and can find shelter behind islands, so driving on the bay feels different from the mangroves. Quick step back. That is two regions down, four to go, and both of them were about nature. The next two run on crime money. Follow the Gulf coast north to Port Gellhorn. Rockstar calls it the forgotten coast of Leonida, a beach town that tourists stopped visiting. They have said very little about it, which makes me want to go there even more. Inland sits Ambrosia, and its official description is my favorite on the entire site. The Allied Crystal Sugar refinery provides the jobs, and the local biker gang provides almost everything else. The rule here is how you get paid. Previews say the map marks gang stashes you can rob. Stolen goods go to a fence, and pawn shops were seen doing that work. Some bank money shows up as marked cash, so you need a special buyer to clean it. For cars, an app called WAINK shows what fences want, how well a car is protected, and whether it has a tracker. Bring it back wrecked and you get paid less. So what is hiding at the top of the map? Mount Kalaga National Park. Trails, forests and fishing. Game Informer says some animals can be tracked and are ranked common, discoverable or legendary, and Rockstar will not say which ones. Panthers? Unconfirmed, but it would be strange to leave them out. The Florida panther has been the state animal since 1982, chosen by a vote of school students. And now the monkeys. In real Florida, rhesus macaques live around Silver Springs in the middle of the state. In 1938 a tour boat operator, Colonel Tooey, released a few on an island to make his jungle cruise more exciting. They swam away almost immediately. So here is my theory. If Rockstar copied real Florida, Leonida's monkeys are not in the Keys. They are inland, somewhere between Ambrosia and Kalaga. That is exactly where I will look first. Now, the part everyone came for, Vice City. Rockstar sums it up in three words, everything to excess. It is Miami, and the details prove it. Ocean Beach has pastel Art Deco hotels. On Rockstar's English site the Little Cuba bakeries keep their Spanish name, panaderias, and the port is the cruise ship capital of the world, which is exactly how real Miami sells itself. Turn on the radio and you will hear Latin stations playing bachata, merengue, dembow and reggaeton. Previews say the city alone is twice the size of Los Santos, and a Rockstar developer told IGN you can rob almost anything. In his demo, it was a headphone store. Which brings us to the big rule of Vice City, the police. That developer says the cops only know about a crime if someone sees it or an alarm goes off. In a city this crowded, someone always sees. So the police build a description of you, and it appears under your stars as icons. Your face, your clothes, your weapon, your car, whether your partner is with you, and whether a camera caught you. Every icon is its own little mission. Change clothes, drop the gun, split up, swap cars, and the pieces of that description start to fade. Previews also say that wearing a mask before a job means they never get your face at all. Be careful with expensive cars, too. Edge says some newer models carry trackers that tell the police where you are. Getting around, you will not need the map much. Previews say you can set a destination without opening the menu, and a ride app called Ride Me works as a fast travel option. Buses and trains actually run on schedules. A Rockstar developer told Edge he checked that buses really reach their stops on time. A service called Scooter Bros will deliver your car for a fee, and the driver leaves on a scooter they brought along. But previews say you start with room for only three cars. Anything beyond that means buying garages. One last thing to watch is the sky. Game Informer confirmed tropical storms and hurricanes, though Rockstar has not said how often they hit. And the map is not just bigger outside. Aaron Garbut told Edge there is an order of magnitude more interiors, which literally means about ten times more doors you can open. The weather also moves across the map. Edge says wind, humidity, fog, time of day, even the season all shape it. After rain, the roads stay wet and dry at different speeds. People on the street run for cover. Boats rock on the waves, and small planes get tossed around and can take minor damage in a bad storm. So yes, flying straight into a hurricane is on my list. Here is my route for night one, based on everything above. Start in the Keys and finish the first missions. Before you do anything stupid, deposit your cash at a bank. Buy a mask and a spare outfit, and keep the clothes in your trunk. Cross into Vice City by bus or train, and save your three garage slots for cars actually worth keeping. Then head north, away from the mission path, while the map is still mostly black. That is Leonida. Six regions, each with its own rules, on a map that only shows you the parts you find yourself. And that pattern I promised? Every region changes one rule of how you play. The Keys teach you about money, Grassrivers about movement, Port Gellhorn and Ambrosia about crime pay, Kalaga about hunting, and Vice City about the police. GTA 6 is out today.
