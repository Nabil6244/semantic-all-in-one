It is almost here. Thirteen years after Los Santos, Grand Theft Auto Six unlocks at midnight on November nineteenth. You already cleared the two hundred and twelve gigabytes of storage space, or the smart two hundred and fifty gigabyte buffer on your SSD, and the second that title screen fades, ninety-nine percent of players are going to do the exact same thing. They are going to sprint straight toward the neon strip of Ocean Beach in Vice City, smash the window of the first parked supercar they see, rob a corner store on the way out, and floor it down the boulevard like it is twenty thirteen all over again.

And within four minutes, that exact GTA Five muscle memory is going to get them hunted across the state of Leonida, stripped of their starting cash, and locked out of early progression. Because according to Rockstar North's developer walkthroughs and hands-on previews, almost every system in GTA Six was engineered around one core rule from Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson: the world no longer forgets what you did the second you break line of sight. From high-end supercars that secretly ping the cops when you slow down for a red light, to a persistent Heat memory layer that tracks the shirt on your back, to Lucia's early ankle-monitor restrictions and cash-gated story chapters, here are the five biggest mistakes ninety-nine percent of players will make in their first hour of GTA Six, and the exact route through Port Gellhorn and the Grassrivers you should take instead.

Let's start with the very first thing everyone does in a Grand Theft Auto game: stealing a parked car. In every previous GTA, boosting a million-dollar hypercar took one button press, a two-second elbow through the glass, and two wires touched together under the steering column. In GTA Six, the one-button hotwire is officially dead. Rockstar has overhauled vehicle theft into a tiered security puzzle. If you walk up to a modern luxury car in Vice City during Hour One and smash the window, two things happen immediately. First, you trip the vehicle alarm. Second, the engine will not turn over because modern keyless ignitions require an electronic key cloner or a relay-attack signal interceptor that you only unlock later through story fences.

Even worse, if you try to bypass the electronics by carjacking a moving supercar at a stoplight, NPCs in Leonida actually fight back. In developer demos, a passenger literally threw the player character straight back out of the driver's seat, while bystanders on the sidewalk immediately pulled out their phones to report your face and license plate. And here is the ultimate Hour One trap: even if you manage to rip a driver out of an exotic car and speed away, slowing down can get you arrested. High-end vehicles and every single police cruiser come wired with built-in GPS trackers. On luxury cars, advanced trackers actually start beeping the moment your speed drops, and if you drive too slowly for too long, the car automatically pings your exact coordinates to Vice City PD.

Here is what you should do instead. Before you ever touch a door handle in Hour One, pull out your in-game phone and open the WAINK scanning app. Scanning any parked car from the sidewalk reveals four critical stats: its lock security tier, alarm status, GPS tracker level, and chop-shop resale value. Until you unlock a fence with a signal jammer who can strip high-tier trackers, stick to older classic sedans and muscle cars that open quietly with a basic slim jim. Just remember to feather the throttle trigger, because rear-wheel-drive muscle cars in GTA Six require real throttle control or they will spin out on wet pavement.

Which brings us to Mistake Number Two: trying to lose the police the old-fashioned way, by ducking into an alleyway for thirty seconds or driving through a Pay and Spray and assuming you are clean. If you do that in GTA Six, you are walking straight into an ambush. First, police officers no longer show up as convenient little cones or dots on your minimap. Instead, you get a red-and-blue directional flash on the edge of your screen warning you which direction sirens are approaching from, forcing you to click into Jason and Lucia's Focus ability to highlight nearby officers in blue through walls.

Second, Rockstar has built an entire Heat memory layer directly underneath your six wanted stars. The stars themselves now have four distinct color states. Hollow stars mean an alarm went off, but police have zero description of who did it, so if you holster your gun and walk normally, you can blend right into the crowd without running. Solid white stars mean officers have direct line of sight on you right now. Flashing white stars mean you just broke line of sight. And red stars mean the active chase is over, but police have locked down a search perimeter and outer ring, and they are hunting for your specific description.

Look right below those stars, and you will see individual evidence icons showing exactly what dispatch knows about you: a camera icon for security footage, a silhouette for your physical build, a weapon icon, a vehicle icon, and a clothing icon. Even if you torch your getaway car or pay for a respray at a Pay and Spray, which also has a cooldown timer now, that only erases the vehicle icon. If the clothing icon is still active, cops patrolling that neighborhood will still recognize the shirt you are wearing twenty minutes later and reignite the pursuit.

Here is the counter-move. The single smartest habit you can build in Hour One is packing a spare outfit inside the trunk of your registered personal vehicle. The moment your stars turn from solid white to flashing, break line of sight, pop your trunk, swap your clothes, drop or stash the weapon witnesses saw, and if you are running a job with both protagonists, split Jason and Lucia up so police looking for a duo overlook a solo pedestrian. And if you swap into an older car the police have never seen before, you can literally drive straight through a police roadblock at normal traffic speed without officers stopping you. Just never abandon your registered personal car next to a crime scene, because Leonida police will treat your car as physical evidence, cordon it off, and tow it to the impound lot.

Mistake Number Three is treating the opening hours like an unrestricted rampage across the map, and accidentally ruining your character's reputation for the rest of your save file. When the story kicks off, you are not living in a penthouse in Downtown Vice City. Jason starts out in a weathered wooden stilt house on the water in Key Lanto down in the Leonida Keys, doing local shakedowns for veteran drug smuggler Brian Heder out of Brian's Boat Works and Marina to pay for rent. Meanwhile, Lucia is wearing an electronic ankle monitor that restricts early-game movement before it is removed and the pair relocates north to the Bayside apartment on Catalan Boulevard in Vice City.

If you ignore the early story boundaries or try to go on a classic GTA rampage in Hour One, you aren't just dealing with beat cops. Leonida fields seven distinct law enforcement agencies, including Vice City PD, Vice Beach PD, Port Gellhorn PD, Leonida Highway Patrol, county sheriffs, marine units, and wildlife poaching patrols, backed by K-9 dogs, helicopter searchlights, armored trucks with mounted guns, and spike strips that are almost invisible at night, scaling all the way up to a returned six-star state-wide manhunt.

Even more importantly, GTA Six tracks a separate Criminal Profile for Jason and for Lucia, running from Professional down through Aggressive, Violent, and Psycho. Unlike Red Dead Redemption Two's Honor bar, this isn't about store discounts. It changes how the world and NPCs react to you. If you shoot compliant store clerks or massacre civilians in free roam during Hour One, you can push your profile into a permanent Shattered state that you can never climb back out of for that entire playthrough. Instead, keep long guns stored in your personal vehicle's trunk until the moment you need them, because walking down the street with an unholstered rifle makes NPCs panic, call nine-one-one, or draw their own concealed weapons. Respect protected crime-free hubs like Drequan's Club and the Jack of Hearts, and use restraint during early stick-ups by firing warning shots to suppress crowds, knocking guards out, or using Focus mode to shoot yellow non-lethal weak points and vehicle tires instead of executing compliant clerks.

Mistake Number Four is how ninety-nine percent of players will go broke in their first two hours: treating GTA Six money like GTA Five money. In GTA Five, you could carry fifty million dollars in your pocket, die in a shootout, and pay a tiny hospital bill, or open the bank website on your phone mid-chase and magically beam paper cash into the cloud. In GTA Six, Wi-Fi cash deposits from your phone are gone. While the BuckMe phone app lets you view your balance, your wealth is split into three strict buckets. First is Bank Money in purple, which is one hundred percent safe, but you can only deposit physical bills by standing at a real-world ATM or walking up to a bank teller. Second is Physical Cash on Hand in green, which is capped at just a few thousand dollars, and if you get busted or killed before reaching an ATM, you lose the cash you are carrying. Third is Duffel Bag Value, which tracks gold bars, diamonds, jewelry, and drugs grabbed during a robbery. Duffel bag loot cannot be spent in stores until you physically transport it to a specialized black-market fence, and if you run a duo robbery with Jason and Lucia and go down, you risk losing both partners' unbanked reserves at once.

On top of that, when you rob a shop in Hour One, do not blow the safe with explosives. Using explosives to crack a safe physically damages the loot inside, slashing your payout. Even worse, while you can smash local security hard drives in dive shops, several upscale Vice City stores back up their CCTV recordings directly to the cloud, meaning you cannot wipe the camera icon off your Heat bar by shooting a monitor in the back office. Instead, use a snap gun to pick back-door locks quietly, disable the alarm box first, and get the safe combination cleanly by searching the manager's desk, intimidating the clerk, or buying the code via text from a fence. Check your getaway vehicle before you start, because motorcycles hold zero duffel bags, standard cars hold two in the trunk, and vans or pickups hold three to five. Then drive straight to an ATM after every single score, because story progression in GTA Six is cash-gated, and main missions alone will not give you enough money to advance.

So if rushing into Downtown Vice City in Hour One gets you tracked by cloud CCTV, pinned down by high-end car trackers, and stuck with luxury loot you don't have a fence for yet, where should you actually go first? First, remember that GTA Six uses a strict Fog-of-War map system where unvisited regions stay completely blank until you physically travel through them, and rushing story markers leaves massive profit zones hidden. Instead of following the crowd into Vice City's camera grid with Jason's starter Garate ES-Nine or Lucia's Nipper Thirty-Eight pistol, take this two-step Hour One route across the southern and western frontier.

Step one: cut through the Leonida Keys mangroves into the Grassrivers. Starting from Brian's boatyard in Key Lanto, learn the two sides of the water in the Leonida Keys. The Atlantic side is deep water built for scuba diving shipwrecks, while the Gulf side is shallow water packed with dense mangroves that act as an instant natural blind spot against police helicopters and patrol boats. From the northern Keys, push straight into the Grassrivers, Leonida's vast, lawless Everglades wetland. Make sure you grab an airboat before entering, because standard hull boats will run aground in the shallow swamp. Head toward the rough waterfront settlement of Watson Bay, where Ultimate Edition players also unlock the Paradise Garage and the off-road nineteen sixty-seven Vapid Dominator buggy. Here in the Grassrivers, there are zero cloud-synced city cameras, police roadblocks cannot follow an airboat through the mangroves, and early wilderness hunting and stilt-shack stashes give you clean starter cash with almost zero Heat.

Step two: push west to Port Gellhorn, Leonida's forgotten coast. Once you clear the swamp, push northwest into Kelly County to Port Gellhorn, a gritty, decaying resort town of cheap motels, closed attractions, and biker bars. Port Gellhorn is the ultimate Hour One goldmine for three reasons. First, it is home to Gellhorn International Raceway, the first full-sized circuit raceway in GTA history, alongside a dedicated drag strip and motocross track. You can farm early circuit races, dirt-bike events, and dive-bar pool and darts for one hundred percent clean, un-marked money while mastering the game's new driving physics. Second, unlike Downtown Vice City banks that require high-tier gear, Port Gellhorn and neighboring Ambrosia are packed with gang strongholds. When you scout a stronghold here, your map actually reveals the exact amount of cash and drugs stored inside before you attack, and stolen drugs can be flipped directly to early black-market buyers, just like the four-thousand-five-hundred-dollar drug fence payout demonstrated in Rockstar's preview. Third, you can hit gritty local targets like Uncle Jack's Liquor and roadside convenience stores using a snap gun and a mask, then bank the physical bills at a local ATM before Port Gellhorn PD can set up a perimeter.

Step three: buy your first storage garage before logging off. Take that first stack of banked Port Gellhorn cash and buy a storage garage immediately, or claim your pre-order Shorecourt Garage near Ocean Beach if you have the Vintage Vice City Pack. Remember, you cannot buy safe houses in GTA Six because safe houses move with the story, so only garages are purchasable. You start the game with only three personal vehicle slots, and any unregistered stolen car will permanently despawn when you close or restart the game unless it is parked inside your garage. Better yet, that garage gives you a dedicated weapon locker and contraband storage so you never lose unfenced jewelry or gold bars if a chase goes sideways.

So before the clock strikes midnight on November nineteenth: scan every car with the WAINK app before touching the door, keep a clean outfit in your trunk to wipe your Heat icons, deposit your pocket cash at physical ATMs after every job, and skip the Hour One Vice City death trap for the Grassrivers and Port Gellhorn money loop.
