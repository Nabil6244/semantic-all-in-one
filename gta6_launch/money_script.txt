One bank. Ten minutes. A hundred and forty thousand dollars, and it isn't even a story mission. Yet GTA 6 caps the cash in your pocket at twenty thousand, and one bust wipes it all out. So how does anyone get rich in a game built to keep you broke? One critic looted two wallets after a shootout and made seventy bucks. The best payday on this list pays two thousand times that. The reviews are in, and critics agree on one thing: this game wants you to feel poor. In GTA 5 you finished the story with tens of millions and nothing left to buy. Here your pocket stops at twenty thousand, getting wasted or busted wipes it, skipping a cab ride costs an extra hundred, and one questline asks you to put away two million. So where does the money come from? I went through the reviews and the launch guides and pulled out fifteen ways to earn in GTA 6, ranked from worthless to the one that really makes you rich. Real numbers where they exist, none invented, and I'll flag any figure that only one outlet reports. Stay for the top three, because the winner isn't what most players would guess. Drop a like, and let's start at the bottom. Number fifteen, fishing. Shacknews counts more than a hundred animals and fish in Leonida that you can catch, hunt, or pet. But the previews were blunt: you can't sell what you catch. Fishing is there to slow you down, not to pay you. If your plan was a quiet, honest life on a boat, that's a hobby, not an income. Dead last. Found a fish that's actually worth money? Tell me in the comments and I'll pin it. Number fourteen, honest work. Sort of. The story starts with Jason in the Leonida Keys, working for Brian Hader, whose boatyard fronts for smuggling. Reviewers describe the early hours as errands for cash tips, and Shacknews calls them odd jobs for next to no pay. One review says a single car repair, about two hundred fifty dollars, can wipe out everything you've saved. That's on purpose. Chapter one restricts how you earn so the game feels like a climb from rags to riches. GTA 5 let you buy a cinema, a cab company, a golf club, and play the stock market. None of the reviews I read mention a stock market here, or any business you can buy. The Sixth Axis says garages are the only property you can own. So do the jobs, because they unlock better tools. They just won't pay your bills. So if honest work is a trap, what about dishonest work? Number thirteen, other people's wallets. In GTA 5, cash burst out of people and glowed on the pavement. Here you search their pockets. Giant Bomb's reviewer finished a parking lot shootout, went through two wallets, and walked away seventy dollars richer. Seventy. You can also hold someone up at gunpoint, and GameSpot's launch guide counts that as an early way to earn. But remember how police work in this game: if anyone sees you, they can call it in. So you're gambling a wanted level, a description of your face, and every dollar in your pocket for lunch money. Number twelve, other people's trunks. You can break into parked cars and take what the owner left inside. A Shacknews reviewer spent half an hour working through every car in one parking lot and came out with weapons, ammunition, cigarettes, hats, sunglasses, a little cash, and drugs. It's a lucky dip. But notice something: most of that isn't money. It's stuff. In GTA 6, stuff only turns into money when somebody agrees to buy it. Hold on to that, because it decides who sits at the top of this list. Number eleven, sunken treasure. The story teaches you to dive in chapter one, then leaves the whole coastline open, with shipwrecks to search whenever you like. What does a wreck pay? Nobody has published a number yet, and I won't invent one. What I can tell you is what Giant Bomb's reviewer found: a stash of drugs in the wreckage of a shark attack, sold for cash. So the water does pay. It just won't tell you how much in advance. Number ten, hunting. This one is confirmed to pay. The previews listed it as a way to earn and the reviews back that up. It opens up as the game does, and Mount Kalaga National Park is where you go. Your kills ride in your trunk like any other loot until you sell them. No reviewer has given a price per animal, but ComicBook says hunting rare wildlife can net big bucks and that you could sink tens of hours into it. Slow, quiet, and the calmest money on this list. Number nine, rob the robbers. Forbes describes it like this: you hear a crash, an explosion, shouting, and you find another crew hitting an armored car. You can take the crew out and leave with what they just stole. Another review says you can sometimes side with the victims and collect a reward instead. Either way the police are coming, and they won't go easy. Nobody has put a number on it, and you can't plan for it. The game decides when to offer it, which is why it isn't higher. Number eight, take the whole ATM. Shacknews counted four hundred fifty-four cash machines in Leonida, and a hundred sixty-eight of them can be hooked to a tow truck. One reviewer with more than two hundred hours calls it free money with one catch: it triggers the wanted system. And here's the irony. That machine is also how you keep your own cash safe, so maybe don't steal the one closest to home. We're halfway, and from here everything pays in thousands. Number seven, the corner store. In GTA 5 you pointed a gun at the clerk, he emptied the register, and that was the entire system. Here Shacknews counted ninety-one variety stores you can rob, before you add pawn shops, liquor stores, and gas stations. The previews estimated a couple thousand dollars a store at most, and nothing in the reviews says otherwise. How you do it changes the take. Get the manager to give up the safe code and you keep everything. Blow the safe and you leave faster with less. Kill the alarm and cameras first and nobody calls it in, and if a camera did catch you, wipe the tapes in the security room so the police have no description. Two warnings. Pawn shops carry marked bills, and one review says dye packs keep ruining them until the story gives you a jammer. And Forbes says cash from a clothing store probably isn't worth the risk. Number six, Pay and Spray. It's more than a paint job now. If you've played GTA Online, you know the idea, Los Santos Customs and one stolen car a day. In GTA 6, Pay and Spray repairs your car, registers a stolen one in your name, and buys the ones you don't want. Shacknews says you can sell almost any vehicle to almost any shop and pocket a couple thousand. So why isn't it higher? Selling only opens later in the story, there's a daily cap on how many cars you can sell, and Game8 says the payout is considerably lower. On top of that, expensive cars carry trackers, and reviews say you have to keep your speed up all the way to the shop. The upside: the better the tracker, the better the price. Which brings us to the people who pay more. Number five, stealing to order. GameSpot's launch guide names two men to meet. First is Moses in Port Gellhorn, who teaches the basics and gives you the tools for mid-range cars. Later comes Randy in Western Vice City, who deals in high-end cars and lets you clone keys, which is how modern ones open. They hand you a list of wanted cars scattered across the map. The price depends on condition, and a car delivered in mint shape earns a bonus. One review says a couple of the right models can bring in a few grand, so use the scanning app on your phone first. It shows what a car sells for, what it costs to register, and whether it has a tracker. Then drive it like it belongs to your grandmother. Number four, the story itself. In GTA 5 the story was your income, and the final heist alone paid tens of millions each. In GTA 6, many of your biggest paydays are still tied to main missions, heists included. There are smuggling runs too. One reviewer flew contraband in a small plane with Jason's friend Donnie. Another damaged the plane on a delivery, and the mission didn't fail: the boss just paid a smaller share to cover the owners. Then there's the bonus almost nobody mentions. The Buckme app sets a savings target for every chapter, and hitting it earns a reward. One review describes a family savings account with targets that start at a hundred thousand dollars, jump to five hundred thousand, and keep climbing, and it says deposits can't be withdrawn. The catch with all of it: finished missions can't be replayed, so you can't farm them. A windfall, not an income. Now, the top three. Number three, jewelry stores. This is the next tier up, and the preview said bigger targets show their estimated take on your map, so you know what a job is worth before you walk in. Forbes says you can sneak in and take everything without tripping a single alarm. But jewelry isn't money. It goes in a bag, the bag goes in your trunk, and a big trunk holds six. Then you need a fence. One review counts three of them, each with a specialty, and what they pay moves with demand. Another says some dealers pay far more than others for the same goods, so compare before you sell. If you're busy, your partner can make the sale for you. One warning: leave loot in a car you haven't registered and you risk losing it. Number two, gang hideouts. The game calls them illegal operations, and they're the closest thing GTA 6 has to a money printer. They aren't marked on your map. You find them by exploring, by overhearing people on the street, or by following a suspicious car that Lucia points out. Inside there's cash, drugs, jewelry, marked bills, and often a rare gun or a one-of-a-kind car. GameSpot expects one or two dozen enemies and calls them fairly easy, while Forbes says they're harder than ninety-five percent of the campaign and warns never to go alone. So bring your partner and bring armor. The safe code might be on a sticky note, on a computer screen, or in another hideout run by the same gang. Sell the goods to a dealer and you're paid almost instantly. PlayStation Universe says stores and hideouts are the only really good ways to earn, and GameSpot says hit them consistently and you'll never worry about money. So what could possibly beat that? Number one, banks. This is the best documented payday in GTA 6 so far, and it comes from Shacknews. The reviewer picked a bank in the heart of the city, with an estimated take of at least a hundred thirty-five thousand dollars. First came body armor and ammunition, then a second vehicle left waiting at a gas station off the freeway. The reviewer followed a guard straight into the vault and took two bags of marked bills, then a third. Out the front door, past dozens of police, onto a sport bike. Back to the gas station, a change of clothes, a different vehicle. An associate bought the bills for a hundred forty thousand dollars. Ten minutes of work. In GTA 5, a bank job was a story mission with a planning board. According to Vice, these aren't story missions at all. You won't pull this on day one, though. The big banks open later, and you need the story's tools first, like that jammer for dye packs. But when someone asks how you make real money in GTA 6, this is the answer. Treat it like a job. So that's the ranking. Fishing pays nothing. Stores pay the bills. Hideouts keep you comfortable. Banks make you rich. And none of it counts until it's banked, because the twenty thousand you're carrying is gone the moment you're wasted or busted, so find an ATM before you find trouble. One last thing, and I'll keep it spoiler-free: two reviews say your money plays a part in how the story ends, and one of them says it's your savings.
