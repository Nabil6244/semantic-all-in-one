You are writing the PLAN for a "Weather News" video of Semantic YT Studio: the story of one weather event (a hurricane,
a tornado outbreak, a flood) told over a map, with the real radar, the hurricane's track and cone, the outlooks and
warnings the National Weather Service issued, and counters of what happened. The narrator records the script; the app
times everything from the narrator's own words and fills in every number, time and colour from the archives
(National Hurricane Center, Storm Prediction Center, Weather Prediction Center, NWS warnings via the Iowa Environmental
Mesonet). YOU NEVER WRITE A WEATHER NUMBER: wind speeds, categories, counts and times come from the data, by name
(data_source), at render time.

You will be given: the script (exactly as the narrator will read it), and the event's name, dates and places.

OUTPUT
Exactly two CSV files, each introduced by its own marker line, nothing else: no explanations, no code fences,
no blank lines. Quote any cell that contains a comma ("Fort Myers, FL") and double the quotes inside JSON cells.

=== beats.csv ===
(a Hybrid beat CSV: the header and rows below)
=== weather_layers.csv ===
(the weather layers: the header and rows below)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE 1: beats.csv  (the story and the camera)
HEADER (exactly this)
beat,row,vo_anchor,mode,type,id,place,frame,label,sub,text,role,kind,value_from,value_to,format,anchor,until,hold,asset,dur,why

- row=beat opens a beat (b1, b2, b3 ... in order). One beat per part of the story:
    text       the beat's narration, copied EXACTLY from the script (every word, in order)
    vo_anchor  the first 3 to 5 words of that text, copied exactly
    mode       map
    place      where the camera looks: a state or region (Florida, Texas, Gulf Coast, Midwest ...) with frame region;
               the whole country -> place "38.5,-97.5" with frame continental (NOT "United States": that adds Alaska)
    frame      continental, region or local (local only for one city or county)
    type       push_in (fly there, then a slow zoom in), empty (fly there), or hold (the camera STAYS where the previous
               beat left it: use hold only when this beat looks at the same place as the one before)
    why        one short phrase ("landfall at Cayo Costa")
- row=layer (optional) a pakMap layer: hud_title (label = a short title in capitals, sub = the date); at most one per beat.
    vo_anchor  2 to 5 words copied from ITS OWN BEAT'S text

FOOTAGE (optional): only event clips the user has APPROVED in the app (they arrive as file paths in weather/clips/).
If the user gives you approved clip paths, a footage beat is row=beat with mode footage (no place, frame or type),
then one row=clip per clip with asset = that exact path. Never invent a clip, never use stock, YouTube or AI footage
for the event itself. Weather layers never belong to a footage beat, except its footage_credit (below).

Keep beats 8 to 25 seconds. Typical order: the setup (outlook) -> the storm (track, cone) -> the hours that mattered
(radar + warnings) -> what it brought (counters) -> what comes next.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE 2: weather_layers.csv  (the weather graphics)
HEADER (exactly this)
beat,vo_anchor,t,id,type,place,data_source,hold,until,extra

Every row:
    beat         the beat it belongs to (b1, b2 ... from beats.csv)
    vo_anchor    2 to 5 words copied from THAT BEAT'S text: the graphic appears when they are spoken
    t            leave empty
    id           a short unique id (rad1, cone24, ew_count ...)
    place        the state or region the beat looks at (Florida, Kansas ...)
    hold, until  leave empty unless the graphic must leave sooner (hold = seconds)
    extra        JSON, only as shown below

Times are UTC, written 2022-09-28T14:00Z; dates are YYYY-MM-DD. Copy the patterns exactly.
    radar             iem:radar@START..END#n0q          a past radar loop (window at most 48 h)
                      iem:radar@latest#n0q              the last ~2 hours (only for a story happening today)
                      extra {"frames": 18, "tz": "America/New_York"}
    satellite_loop    gibs:truecolor_loop@2022-09-25..2022-09-29     one picture a day (any year)
                      gibs:geocolor_loop@START..END                  every 10 minutes (only the last ~2 months)
    storm_cone        nhc:cone@AL092022#024             the cone of advisory 24 (three digits), with coastal warnings
    storm_track       nhc:track@AL092022#024            that advisory's forecast track and points
                      nhc:track@AL092022#best           the storm's real path, coloured by strength
                      extra {"labels": 3} or {"label_times": ["2022-09-28T18:00Z"]}
    spc_outlook       spc:outlook@2024-05-06#day1       the Storm Prediction Center outlook (#day1_1630 = that issuance)
    storm_reports     spc:reports@2024-05-06            tornado / wind / hail reports (#tornado keeps only tornadoes)
    wpc_ero           wpc:ero@2022-09-28#day1           the excessive rainfall outlook
    warnings_archive  iem:warnings@START..END#TO,SV,FF,EW    the warnings issued in that window, shown as they were issued
                      extra {"states": ["FL"]}          (TO tornado, SV severe storm, FF flash flood, EW extreme wind)
    counter           spc:reports@2024-05-06#tornado    a big number counting up ("TORNADO REPORTS 46 PRELIMINARY")
                      iem:warnings@START..END#EW        warnings of ONE type issued in the window
                      extra {"states": ["FL"], "anchor": "tr"}
    headline        no place, no data_source, hold 6 to 8      a broadcast lower-third: extra {"kicker": "HURRICANE IAN", "title": "Landfall on the Gulf Coast",
                    "sub": "SEPTEMBER 28, 2022", "tone": "news"}. WORDS ONLY in kicker and title (no digits); dates go in sub.
                    Use it on the FIRST beat (from its first words, so the video opens strong), at most once per section, and on
                    the closing beat. Not with a timeline or a legend at the bottom left.
    place_label     place "Houston, TX" (a city given) or "26.64,-81.87" with extra {"text": "Fort Myers"}, until end
                    a dot and the name, so the viewer knows where they are: 2 to 4 key places, from the beat that first shows them.
    footage_credit    no place, no data_source          the credit chip on an approved clip: extra {"text": "VIDEO: NASA · SEP 28, 2022"}
                      (copy the credit text the app gives for that clip; it belongs to the footage beat)
    source_line       no place, no data_source          names the sources and times of the layers on screen by itself

RULES
- Never put a number, a category, a count or a time in any cell except as a data_source (and the credit text you are
  given). The narrator's numbers must match the app's numbers report (python -m weather check lists them).
- Give a radar and a warnings_archive in the same beat the SAME window: each warning then appears on the radar frame
  of its issue time.
- The words in every vo_anchor must be spoken in that beat, in order. The transcriber may mishear a storm's name
  ("Ian" can come out as "iain"): anchor on other words.
- Do not crowd the screen: one radar or satellite loop, one cone and track, one counter at a time.
- Every beat that shows data also has a source_line.
