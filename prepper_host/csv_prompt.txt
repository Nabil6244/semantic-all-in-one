You are writing a Prepper Host CSV for the "Prepper Host" style of Semantic YT Studio: a talking-host prepper explainer in
the style of "90 Days Without Power: the exact cart I'd buy". One host (default: Dale, a rancher in his late fifties) talks
to camera in his workshop; the video cuts every 3-6 seconds between him, split screen, a round host cam over b-roll,
real-life b-roll pictures, product photo cards and number cards.

Write ONE CSV (comma separated, double quotes around any cell with a comma) with this header, exactly:
line,shot,visual,product,title,rows,chapter

RULES
- `line` is the narration, word for word, in order. All lines together ARE the voiceover. First person, calm, plain words,
  short sentences, real prices and quantities. One or two sentences per row (about 4-9 seconds spoken).
- `shot` (leave empty to let the director pick):
    host       the host talking to camera (open with 1-2 of these, and use one every ~10 rows)
    split      host on the left, the `visual` picture on the right
    pip        the `visual` picture full screen with a round host cam top right
    broll      the `visual` picture full screen (a slow camera move is added)
    product    one product photo card: `product` = the product name (brand + item, e.g. "Kirkland Signature 40-pack bottled water")
    compare    2-3 product cards side by side: `product` = "Rice | Pinto beans | Rolled oats", `rows` = labels "RICE; BEANS; OATS"
    data       math rows: `rows` = "4 PEOPLE x 90 DAYS = 1 GAL/DAY; WATER YOU NEED = 360 GAL !red"
    checklist  ticked list: `rows` = "PORCH; OPEN GARAGE; ~THE ATTIC; -BACKYARD"   (~ = struck out in red, - = no tick)
    bars       bar chart: `rows` = "CASES = 100 GAL; JUGS = 35 GAL; WATER HEATER = 50 GAL" (the numbers set the bar lengths)
    big        one big number: `rows` = "360 GAL = WATER FOR A FAMILY OF FOUR"
    chapter    a bumper with `title` ("ITEM 2 · FOOD") over a b-roll of this line's `visual`
    thumbnail  (one row, no line) `title` = 2 short lines "90 DAYS | NO POWER", `visual` = the thumbnail picture, `product` = what he holds
- `visual` = a picture prompt for split / pip / broll / chapter rows: a concrete real-life scene, place, light and objects.
  Write {host} wherever the host is in the picture: "{host} lifting a case of water onto a garage shelf, afternoon light".
  Mix: the host doing the thing (~40 %), empty-store / dark-house / family-by-lantern moments, close-ups of the items.
- `title` = the chip on a card, 2-4 words in capitals ("THE WATER MATH", "NEVER STORE IT HERE", "THE BIG THREE").
- `rows` = the card rows (see shot); keep each label 1-4 words, values short ("360 GAL", "$5", "~40,000 CAL"). Colours:
  !red for the scary number, !green for the good result, !yellow for a total.
- `chapter` = optional bumper text when the list moves on ("ITEM 3 · COOKING").
- Every number spoken gets a data / big / bars row; every named product gets a product or compare row; never two cards
  in a row more than twice; the host (host / split / pip) at least every 3 rows.
- Structure (about 25 minutes = ~190-230 rows; a test can be 15-30 rows):
  1. Cold open: news hook, the scary number, the promise ("one cart, one family of four, 90 days, item by item, with the
     price and the reason") and a tease for the end ("the three things I'd never buy").
  2. One authority line about the host (his ranch years) and a folksy rule.
  3. Items in the order he'd buy them: what, exact store item + price, the math, why, one trick most people miss.
  4. "People do the opposite… don't be that family."
  5. A normal day of meals from the cart (breakfast, lunch, dinner) to prove it is livable.
  6. The promised list of things he'd never buy.
  7. The small budget version, one thing to do this week, a neighbour line, a comment question, subscribe line, title callback.
- Output only the CSV, no commentary.
