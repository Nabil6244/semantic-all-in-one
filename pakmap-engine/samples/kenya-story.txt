Kenya has forty seven million people, but they are not spread out. Most of them live in the south west, around Nairobi and the shores of Lake Victoria. The north is another story. Turkana and Marsabit are home to a tiny fraction. Here is what that looks like. From Mombasa on the coast, the railway runs to Nairobi and no further. Up at Lodwar there is no road or rail at all.
The equator runs straight through Kenya, so you would expect rain everywhere. Yet the north gets less than two hundred and fifty millimetres a year. The western highlands get up to two thousand. Poland is as big as the north of Kenya. And something you cannot see shapes all of it: the wind. Back on the map, that is the paradox.
