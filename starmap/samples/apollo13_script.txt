In April 1970, Apollo 13 set out for the Fra Mauro highlands of the Moon. Within twelve minutes the crew was circling the Earth, and less than three hours later they were on their way. More than two days into the flight, over 300,000 kilometres from home, an oxygen tank exploded. The landing was off. Using the lunar module as a lifeboat, they fired its engine to swing back onto a path around the Moon. They looped behind the far side, 254 kilometres above the surface, farther from Earth than any crew had ever travelled. Two hours later they fired the lunar module's engine again to speed up the trip home. On the seventeenth of April, Mission Control watched the screens and waited. Odyssey splashed down in the Pacific near the USS Iwo Jima, and all three astronauts came home.
