In December 1968, three astronauts left Florida on a mission no one had ever flown. A Saturn V carried them off the pad, with the Moon already waiting in the sky. Within twelve minutes Apollo 8 was circling the Earth, 185 kilometres up. Less than three hours after launch, the third stage fired again and sent them out of orbit, towards the Moon. For almost three days they coasted across nearly 400,000 kilometres of empty space. On Christmas Eve they slipped behind the Moon and fired their engine to stay in lunar orbit. On the fourth orbit, Bill Anders looked up and saw the Earth rising over the lunar horizon. After ten orbits they fired the engine once more, early on Christmas Day, and headed home. Two days later Apollo 8 splashed down in the Pacific, near the USS Yorktown.
