In July 1969, three astronauts set out from Florida to land on the Moon. A Saturn V rocket lifted them off the pad with the force of a small earthquake. Within twelve minutes Apollo 11 was circling the Earth, 190 kilometres up. Then the third stage fired again and pushed the spacecraft out of orbit, towards the Moon. For three days the crew coasted across almost 400,000 kilometres of empty space. On the nineteenth of July they slipped behind the Moon and fired their engine to enter lunar orbit. The next day Eagle separated from Columbia and dropped toward the Sea of Tranquility. With seconds of fuel to spare, Eagle touched down. Six hours later, Armstrong stepped onto the Moon.
