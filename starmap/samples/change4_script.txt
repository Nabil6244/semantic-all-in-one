In December 2018, China launched Chang'e-4 from Xichang, aiming for a place no spacecraft had ever landed: the far side of the Moon. Less than five days later it was in orbit around the Moon, 100 kilometres up. The far side never faces the Earth, so a relay satellite called Queqiao waited 65,000 kilometres beyond the Moon to pass on its signals. On the third of January 2019, the lander dropped towards Von Karman crater. It touched down at 10:26 in the morning, Beijing time. A few weeks later, NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter photographed the lander from above. On the night of the landing, its rover, Yutu-2, rolled onto the floor of the crater.
