On the fourteenth of July 2023, India launched Chandrayaan-3 from Sriharikota. Instead of flying straight to the Moon, it spent more than two weeks raising its orbit around the Earth, one burn at a time. On the first of August it left Earth behind, and five days later it was captured by the Moon's gravity. Over the next eleven days its orbit was lowered, until it circled about 150 kilometres above the surface. No spacecraft had ever landed so close to the Moon's south pole. Six days after separating from its propulsion module, the Vikram lander began its descent. At three minutes past six in the evening, Indian time, on the twenty-third of August, Vikram touched down. India named the landing site the Shiv Shakti point.
